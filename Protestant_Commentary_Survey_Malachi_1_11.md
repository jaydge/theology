## 1. Method, and the two buckets as JD defined them

JD's controlling question, in his own framing: the concern is **the actual burning of physical incense** as an element of New Testament worship, introduced without command, apostolic example, or good and necessary consequence. He asked for two buckets only, with Eucharistic readings treated as a subset of "not literal incense," and separately asked whether *anyone at all* pairs a Eucharistic *minchah* with physical burning of incense.

- **BUCKET A** — the verse predicts, commands, warrants, or yields as a good and necessary consequence the **actual burning of physical incense** as Christian worship.
- **BUCKET B** — not that: the incense clause is read figuratively (prayer, praise, spiritual worship), whether or not the *pure offering* is given a Eucharistic sense.

⭐⭐ **A THIRD DISTINCTION IS INTRODUCED HERE THAT THE SUBMITTED TABLE DID NOT MAKE, AND IT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT METHODOLOGICAL ADDITION OF THIS PASS.** Within Bucket B, this file separates:

- **B-explicit** — the commentator **affirmatively rejects** a material/physical reading of the incense clause, or names and refuses the inference to church ceremonial. *An argument.*
- **B-silent** — the commentator reads the clause figuratively and **simply never addresses** whether physical incense may be burned in churches. *Not an argument — an absence of one.*

⛔⛔ **THIS MATTERS FOR DEPLOYMENT AND THE BRIEF ASKED FOR IT EXPLICITLY. Counting a B-silent commentator as though he had denied physical incense is the same error as reading Rev. James's silence as agreement** — the distinction the previous pass had to draw. A commentator who says "incense here means prayer" has told you what the verse means. He has not told you what he thinks about a thurible, and he may never have been asked. **Six of the seventeen are B-explicit. Eleven are B-silent.** Both are Bucket B; only the six are evidence of a *position*.

## 2. The seventeen pre-1900 Protestant commentators, verified

Each entry: tier, the reading as verified from the source's own words, a supporting quotation held under 15 words per the brief's copyright rule (or a paraphrase where the sense needs more), the bucket, and any divergence from the submitted table.

### 1. Geneva Bible notes (Reformed, 1560/1599) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, "Geneva Study Bible")
The note glosses the verse as the calling of the Gentiles, and says the prophet used words the people would understand: by altar and sacrifice **he means the spiritual service of God under the Gospel**, all legal ceremonies being ended by Christ's sacrifice alone.
- Quotation: *"by the altar and sacrifice he means the spiritual service of God"* (11 words).
- **BUCKET B — B-silent.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 2. John Calvin (Reformed, 1559) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, Calvin's *Commentaries*)
Calvin reads incense and *minchah* as a common prophetic idiom: the Prophets, writing under the law, accommodated their language to the people, so "an altar shall be built to God" means spiritual worship. He states flatly that the New Testament sacrifices are prayer and thanksgiving, per Hebrews 13.
- Quotation: *"We do not find there any incense or sacrifices."* (8 words.)
- ⭐ **STRONGER THAN THE SUBMITTED TABLE RECORDED.** Calvin adds a line the table omitted and which is squarely on JD's regulative-principle concern: whoever builds an altar for himself **subverts the cross of Christ**, on which the only true and perpetual sacrifice was offered. He also attacks the Papists by name for inferring from this verse that God cannot be worshipped without a sacrifice.
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED and understated.**

### 3. The Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Art. XXIV (Lutheran, 1531) — **Tier 1** (CCEL, complete Part 30; corroborated at bookofconcord.org)
The Apology cites Malachi 1:11 among the sacrifices of the New Testament and says the adversaries **perversely apply the passage to the Mass**. Its own reading: incense and a pure offering signify not a ceremony worked *ex opere operato* but all those sacrifices through which the Lord's name becomes great — faith, invocation, preaching of the Gospel, confession.
- Quotation: *"incense and a pure offering signify not a ceremony ex opere operato"* (11 words).
- ⚠️⚠️ **DIVERGENCE — A CONCESSION THE SUBMITTED TABLE OMITTED, AND IT IS NOT TRIVIAL.** The Apology continues: **if anyone would have the term embrace the ceremony, "we readily concede it"** — provided he neither understands the ceremony alone nor teaches that it profits *ex opere operato*. The table rendered this as "ceremony only as thanksgiving," which is true but softer than the text. The Apology explicitly allows the *pure offering* to include the Lord's Supper. ⛔ **It still says nothing whatever in favour of burning physical incense, and the concession is about the ceremony of the Mass, not the censer.**
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit.** ⚠️ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED but INCOMPLETE.**

### 4. John Trapp (Puritan/Anglican, c. 1660) — **Tier 2** (StudyLight, *Commentary on the Old and New Testaments*)
Trapp quotes Bellarmine's use of the verse for the Mass — *insigne testimonium pro sacrificio Missae* — and rejects it, taking the meaning to be the spiritual sacrifices of the New Testament which the whole royal priesthood offers.
- Quotation: *"This incense is prayer and praise"* (6 words).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **THE SINGLE MOST VALUABLE ENTRY IN THE SET FOR JD'S CROSS-TEXT QUESTION, AND THE SUBMITTED TABLE DID NOT NOTICE IT.** Trapp yokes **all four** of the texts ritualists cite together in one sentence and reads **all four figuratively**: he calls incense and offering "the type for the thing thereby shadowed," citing **Revelation 5:8 and Revelation 8:3-4**, and glosses the incense as prayer and praise at **Psalm 141:2** with Hebrews 13:15. ⭐ **He also attributes that reading to Irenaeus, Tertullian and Augustine.** So the ritualist proof-text *cluster* is not merely unaddressed by the older Protestants; here it is assembled and answered as a cluster, in 1660.
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED, and materially understated.**

### 5. Matthew Poole (Reformed, 1685) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Annotations*)
Reads the verse as the promise that God will cast off ceremonial services and carnal ordinances and set up spiritual worship, summed up in John 4:21-24.
- Quotation: *"Incense; a law term for a gospel duty"* (8 words).
- **BUCKET B — B-silent.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 6. Matthew Henry (Presbyterian/Nonconformist, 1706) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub; ⭐ the **full** *Exposition*, MHCW, not only the Concise)
⚠️ **The submitted table cited Henry 1706, i.e. the full commentary; the readily-available text is the Concise. Both were retrieved and read. They agree, and the full text is stronger.** Henry says that in place of the carnal ordinances the priests profaned, incense shall be offered — signifying prayer and praise, with Psalm 141:2 and Revelation 8:3 cited — **instead of** the blood and fat of bulls and goats, and that the offering is refined not only from the priests' corruptions but **from the mere bodily exercise that was in the institutions themselves**.
- Quotation: *"Incense shall be offered to God's name, which signifies prayer and praise"* (12 words).
- ⭐ **NOTE FOR JD'S RPW CONCERN:** Henry's *"instead of"* and his phrase about bodily exercise make this a substitution reading, not merely a symbolic one. The material rite is not re-described; it is replaced.
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-silent** (he does not address church censing; he does exclude the material institutions).
- ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 7. John Gill (Particular Baptist, 1748–63) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Exposition*)
Prayer in every place is what is meant, "comparable to incense for its fervency, fragrancy, and gratefulness," with Psalm 141:2 cited; the pure offering is the Gentiles themselves, or their praise, good works and alms, per Hebrews 13:15.
- Quotation: *"comparable to incense for its fervency, fragrancy, and gratefulness"* (8 words).
- **BUCKET B — B-silent.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 8. John Wesley (Methodist, 1765) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Explanatory Notes*)
- Quotation: *"A law term for a gospel duty"* (7 words).
- ⛔⛔ **DIVERGENCE — THIS IS NOT AN INDEPENDENT WITNESS. Wesley's note is Poole's note** (entry 5), in substantially identical words, consistent with the *Explanatory Notes* being an avowed abridgement of earlier expositors. **The submitted table lists them as two of seventeen without noting the dependence.** ⭐ **The reading is correctly recorded; the independence is not.** See §0's structural caveat.
- **BUCKET B — B-silent.** ⚠️ **Submitted table: reading CONFIRMED; independence NOT ESTABLISHED.**

### 9. Joseph Benson (Methodist, 1811–18) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Commentary*)
Says the prophet describes the Christian sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving **by the outward rites of the Jewish worship**, that incense was reckoned by the Jews themselves an emblem of prayer (Ps 141:2; Lk 1:10), and that the verse declares two points in the fullest manner: the abolition of the ancient sacrifices and ceremonies, and the spiritual nature of Christian worship.
- Quotation: *"Prayers and praises shall be presented before me as incense"* (10 words).
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit** (the abolition of the ceremonies is stated as one of the verse's two express teachings).
- ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 10. Adam Clarke (Methodist, 1832) — **Tier 1** (sacred-texts.com, *Commentary on the Bible*, complete Malachi 1)
- Quotation: *"The total abolition of the Mosaic sacrifices... is here foretold"* (9 words, elided).
- Clarke: the incense of praise and the pure offering of the Lamb, and through him a holy loving heart, presented everywhere among the Gentiles.
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit** (abolition stated as the verse's content).
- ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 11. Charles Simeon (Anglican evangelical, 1832) — **Tier 1** (StudyLight, *Horae Homileticae*, Discourse 1269 "The Calling of the Gentiles", continuous text)
⭐ Simeon argues the verse **implies the intended abolition of the Mosaic law**: sacrifices and incense were to be offered at Jerusalem alone, so a prophecy of incense and offerings in every place cannot stand without disannulling that commandment — and with the one commandment, the whole law, the priestly office being superseded. He reasons the point explicitly by analogy to Hebrews 7:11-14 on the Melchizedek priesthood.
- Quotation: *"It is the incense of a devout spirit... that God approves"* (10 words, elided).
- ⭐⭐ **NOTE FOR JD:** Simeon's is the clearest *argued* form of the abolition reading in the whole set — it is a deduction with named premises, not an assertion, and its structure ("a prophecy of X in every place entails the repeal of the commandment confining X to one place") is directly transferable.
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED and understated.**

### 12. Albert Barnes (Presbyterian, 1870) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Notes*)
Barnes reads the *minchah* as a special sacrifice which, since the daily meal-offering accompanied the lamb typifying Christ's blood-shedding, was **typical of the holy eucharist**. He assembles Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian and Hippolytus at length. From Irenaeus he reproduces the identification of the incense with the prayers of the saints in the Apocalypse.
- Quotation: *"incense, John in the Apocalypse declares to be the prayers of the Saints"* (12 words — Irenaeus, quoted by Barnes).
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B, with a Eucharistic *minchah*.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.** ⭐ **This is the pairing JD asked after — Eucharistic offering + incense-as-prayer — and it is the exact inverse of the ritualist pairing.**

### 13. Jamieson, Fausset & Brown (Presbyterian, 1871) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub)
- Quotation: *"The 'incense' is figurative of prayers"* (5 words), citing Ps 141:2 and Rev 8:3.
- JFB allow the Lord's Supper reference "maintained by many of the fathers," but only as a *spiritual* offering accepted through the once-slain Lamb.
- **BUCKET B — B-silent.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 14. Keil & Delitzsch (Lutheran, 1866–68) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Biblical Commentary on the OT*)
Reads the verse prophetically of the spread of the kingdom among the nations, *"in every place"* standing against the one place of Deuteronomy 12.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **THE MOST IMPORTANT SINGLE CORRECTION IN §2, AND IT STRENGTHENS THE SURVEY.** The submitted table credited the *symmetry argument* — that you may not materialise one clause and spiritualise the other — to Perowne alone. **It is in Keil & Delitzsch too, in a footnote, and stated as a rule of construction:** the literal interpretation of *minchâh* is **precluded by the parallel "burning incense"**; if burning incense was a symbol of prayer, then the sacrificial offering can only have denoted the spiritual surrender of a man to God (Rom 12:1). ⭐ **So the survey's sharpest interpretive instrument is attested twice, independently, in two traditions (Lutheran 1866, Anglican 1890) — not once.**
- Quotation: *"the literal interpretation of the word is precluded by the parallel 'burning incense'"* (12 words).
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED, and materially understated.**

### 15. Pulpit Commentary (W. J. Deane, Anglican, 1880s) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub)
- Quotation: *"Incense is to our minds a type of prayer"* (8 words), citing Rev 5:8 and Rev 8:3.
- The pure oblation is the symbol of the Christian sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving; Deane records that the Fathers, mediaeval writers **and many modern commentators** see a prophecy of the Holy Eucharist here, without adopting it as the ground of the incense clause.
- **BUCKET B, noting the Eucharistic reading — B-silent.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

### 16. C. J. Ellicott (Anglican, 1882) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub, *Commentary for English Readers*)
The verse is a prophetic announcement of the calling of the Gentiles; *"in every place"* stands against Deuteronomy 12:5-7, with John 4:21-24 compared.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **THE REDUCTIO IS REAL AND IT IS VERBATIM.** Ellicott: if any Christians would claim this verse as support for their custom of offering incense in churches, **they must conform also with Zechariah 14:16-21 and go up every year to Jerusalem to keep the Feast of Tabernacles.**
- Quotation: *"they must conform also with Zechariah 14:16-21"* (7 words).
- ⚠️⚠️ **BUT A HEDGE THE SUBMITTED TABLE DID NOT REPORT, AND IT IS THE KIND A RITUALIST WILL FIND.** Ellicott's spiritual reading is stated **conditionally**: *"unless we are to expect some future establishment of a universal offering of material sacrifices, we must understand both expressions in a spiritual sense."* ⛔ **He does not close the future-material door; he brackets it.** He does not himself expect it, and calls the spiritual sense "the only reasonable way" — but the protasis is his own, and it is precisely the door the millennialists in §5 walk through.
- ⚠️ **Also worth knowing: Ellicott is the source of the pointer to Pusey.** He commends Pusey's footnote and lists the Fathers Pusey gathers. He does this while disagreeing with the Eucharistic conclusion — *"Those who prefer so-called authority to the results of calm criticism are bound to disagree with us."*
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit, and the strongest anti-ritualist note in the set.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED, with an unreported hedge.**

### 17. Cambridge Bible for Schools and Colleges (T. T. Perowne, Anglican, 1890) — **Tier 2** (BibleHub)
Incense and offering are the spiritual sacrifices of prayer and praise (Heb 13:15), almsgiving (Heb 13:16; Phil 4:18) and self-dedication (Rom 12:1), which all Christians as a holy priesthood offer.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **PEROWNE'S TRAP IS REAL, VERBATIM, AND IS THE DIRECT ANSWER TO JD'S "ANYBODY AT ALL" QUESTION.** He states it as a canon of interpretation: if the *minchah* means the bread and wine of the Lord's Supper, then **we have here a prophecy of the universal offering of literal incense also**, for by no sound canon may we give a material sense to one and a figurative sense to the other of two words placed in the same category by a writer. And then, he says, **it follows that incense is as necessary a part of Christian worship as the bread and wine.** He refuses the move.
- Quotation: *"incense is as necessary a part of Christian worship as 'the bread and wine'"* (13 words).
- ⭐ **Perowne also pre-empts the patristic appeal**, arguing that it has been "too hastily assumed" that the early Christian writers put the Eucharistic interpretation on the *minchah*, and reading Justin against that assumption from Justin's own qualification that prayers and thanksgivings are the only perfect sacrifices.
- **BUCKET A: NO. BUCKET B — B-explicit.** ✅ **Submitted table: CONFIRMED.**

They avoid the conclusion only by a dispensational scheme you do not hold."* ⛔ **For a non-dispensationalist — which the Reformed side of this argument is — Gaebelein and Constable are NOT safe witnesses. They are, on the literal-incense question, closer to the ritualist than to Calvin.** Their value is narrow and should be stated narrowly: they establish that even readers who take the verse's cultic nouns literally **do not** locate that fulfilment in the church.

✅ **Coffman is the one modern witness that is clean for JD's purposes**, reading the incense as the prayers of the saints and the pure offering as praise and a holy life, with no future-material remainder.

⭐ **REVISED MODERN COUNT: 0 of 4 for church-age physical incense. But 2 of 4 (probably 3) read the verse as predicting literal incense somewhere.** The submitted table's flat "0% / 100%" is not sustainable without that qualification.

## 6. The other texts ritualists cite, in the same commentators

JD asked whether the same commentaries should be checked on Revelation 5:8, Revelation 8:3-4 and Psalm 141:2, since ritualists cite these alongside Malachi. They were.

### 6a. Psalm 141:2 — **uniform, and unhelpful to the ritualist**
Calvin (Tier 2): the legal ceremonies taught God's people to reckon their prayers as acceptable to God as the sweetest incense; the help was **typical**, and he does not carry the ceremony forward. Gill: incense was an emblem of prayer offered on the altar Christ — ⭐ **and Gill himself cross-references Malachi 1:11 at this verse**, which is a genuine internal link in the tradition. Poole: God accepts the prayer *instead of* the incense, David being banished from the altar. Barnes, Ellicott, Cambridge, Pulpit: all figurative.
⚠️ **One technical caveat, recorded because it cuts slightly against the simplest form of JD's grammatical argument.** Keil & Delitzsch observe that **the psalmist purposely omits the כּ of comparison** — he does not say "like incense," he asks God to regard the prayer *as* incense. K&D still read it as prayer, not ceremony. But *"the grammar is simile, not institution"* is a shade too quick as stated in the submitted survey. **The grammar is not a plain simile; the figurative reading rests on sense and context, which is sufficient, but it should be argued rather than asserted from the particle.**

### 6b. Revelation 5:8 — **identification, and it is unanimous**
Ellicott, Henry, Barnes, Gill, Poole, JFB, Cambridge, Pulpit and the Geneva notes all read the golden vials of odours as the prayers of the saints. Barnes: incense was a proper emblem of prayer, acceptable and ascending. JFB adds that this gives no sanction to praying to saints.
⚠️ **Caveat, and it is against JD's strongest form.** The Cambridge Bible notes that **if the strict grammar is pressed, it is the *vials*, not the *odours*, that are identified with the prayers.** ⛔ **The "which are the prayers of the saints" identity argument is therefore not as airtight grammatically as the submitted survey implied.** It is still the unanimous reading of the commentators; it is not a knock-down grammatical point.

### 6c. Revelation 8:3-4 — ⛔⛔ **THE COMMENTATORS SPLIT, AND ONE OF THEM HELPS THE RITUALIST**
This is the passage the submitted survey handled least well.
- **Reading 1 — the angel is Christ, the incense is his merit and intercession.** Gill (at length), Poole (*"by this angel I understand Christ"*), the Geneva notes (*"the great emperor, the Lord Jesus Christ"*). On this reading the "much incense" is Christ's mediation added to the saints' prayers, and there is nothing for a church to imitate.
- **Reading 2 — the angel is a created angel; the incense is something *given* to him and *added* to the prayers.** **Ellicott**: the incense was added to give fragrance to the prayers and render them acceptable, though he warns that doctrinal inferences from metaphors are always to be suspected. JFB: the angel does not provide the incense, Christ's merit is the incense. Pulpit: a created angel taking part in the prayers.
- ⚠️⚠️⚠️ **AND THE CAMBRIDGE BIBLE GOES FURTHER, IN THE RITUALIST'S DIRECTION, AND IT MUST BE REPORTED.** Cambridge at Rev 8:3 says the image is that the prayers of the saints are **already lying on the altar**, and the angel — **"in modern liturgical phrase, 'censes the holy things'"** — and expressly distinguishes this from Revelation 5:8, **"where the incense is the prayers of the Saints, not something added to them."**
- ⛔⛔ **THAT IS, PRECISELY, THE RITUALIST'S REV 8 ARGUMENT, MADE BY A PRE-1900 ANGLICAN COMMENTARY IN THIS VERY SET — the same series, and the same volume-family, as Perowne.** It is a comment on the vision's imagery, not a warrant for parish censing, and Cambridge draws no such conclusion. ⭐ **But the submitted survey's claim that "identity language works against 'incense plus prayers as two elements'" is TRUE OF REV 5:8 AND EXPRESSLY DENIED OF REV 8:3 by one of its own seventeen sources.** JD should not deploy that claim in its general form. If it is deployed at all, it must be confined to Revelation 5:8, and he should expect Cambridge on Revelation 8:3 to be quoted back at him.

⭐ **NET RESULT ON THE CROSS-TEXTS.** The older Protestants read all three figuratively and none of them treats any of the three as instituting a rite. **But the ritualist's Revelation 8 move — incense *with* prayers, as distinct from incense *as* prayers — is not a misreading these commentators uniformly foreclose. One of them makes it.** That is a real, if bounded, weakness in the survey as submitted, and it is better found here than across the table from Rev. James.

## 7. The 1559 claim — ⛔ **NOT SUPPORTED AS FRAMED**

The tactical memo flagged this claim as the higher-risk one: *"the 1559 claim especially needs checking; 'outlawed incense' is a strong reading the Ornaments Rubric material complicates."* **The memo was right, and the complication is worse than it anticipated — some of the evidence runs the other way.**

**What was checked, and at what tier.**
⚠️ **Open-web retrieval on this question was poor and is reported as such rather than dressed up.** The Elizabethan Injunctions could not be obtained in full text from Project Canterbury, British History Online, or archive.org within this pass. ⛔⛔ **One reported claim that the 1559 Injunctions permit worship "with a whiff of incense" was traced only to a modern scholarly paraphrase whose source document could not be reached. IT IS RECORDED HERE AS UNVERIFIED AND IS NOT RELIED ON. It may be sound; nothing in this file rests on it.**

**What is established, and most of it from the repository's own holdings.**

1. ⛔ **NO 1559 INSTRUMENT EXAMINED NAMES INCENSE.** Not the Ornaments Rubric, not the Ornaments Proviso of the Act of Uniformity, not the Advertisements of 1566. The prohibition, if there is one, is an **inference from a general rule about unauthorised ceremonies** — which is in fact how JD stated the claim to begin with ("not an existing authorized ceremony"). ⭐ **His reasoning is closer to the sources than his verb is. "Outlawed" asserts a naming that did not happen.**
2. ⭐⭐ **THE PROVISO IS PROVISIONAL, AND THE PROJECT ALREADY HOLDS THE PROOF.** `Ritualist_Case_For_Incense_and_the_1899_Opinion.md` §4b records that the Westall submission quotes the operative clause **twice**, at printed pp. 15 and 36, inside Sandys' letter to Parker of 30 April 1559: ornaments are retained ***"until it please the Queen to take other order for them."*** A retention clause with a suspensive condition is not an outlawing.
3. ⛔⛔ **AND THE REPOSITORY HOLDS EVIDENCE POINTING THE OPPOSITE WAY, WHICH IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING IN THIS SECTION.** The same file records **the Poole inventory of 1559 listing censers in a parish certified as conforming to the Prayer Book and Injunctions**, and the **Bodmin indenture of 1566 committing a censer to the churchwardens "to be used and occupied."** ⚠️ **This does not establish continuous ceremonial use — the file is careful about that at its §7b(3) — but a parish certified as *conforming* while holding censers is very hard to square with "1559 outlawed incense."**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **THE STRONGEST VERSION OF THE PROHIBITION ARGUMENT IS ALREADY IN THE REPOSITORY, AND IT COMES FROM THE RITUALISTS' OWN ADVOCATE — BUT IT IS EDWARDIAN, NOT 1559.** The file records T. A. Lacey printing counter-evidence against himself: **Ridley's own Injunction restrains the minister to "only the ceremonies and gestures appointed by the Book of Common Prayer, and none other,"** and Lacey concedes: ***"Strictly interpreted this would exclude the use of Incense."*** ⭐ **That is the mechanism JD's claim describes — exclusion by a general authorisation rule rather than by name — conceded by the other side. It is worth far more than the 1559 framing, and it is already verified in the project's own file.**
5. ⚠️ The 1899 Archbishops themselves conceded the 1559 Act *"was imperfectly obeyed"* and that there was *"a certain amount of conscious and still more unconscious disobedience"* (recorded at the same file's §7).

⭐ **WHAT THE SOURCES ACTUALLY SUPPORT, stated separately from the submitted claim's framing:**

> No Elizabethan instrument examined names incense. The 1559 settlement retained pre-existing ornaments provisionally, "until other order" be taken by the Queen, and censers demonstrably remained in at least some parishes certified as conforming. The argument that incense was thereby excluded is an argument from a general rule confining ministers to appointed ceremonies — an argument whose strongest form is Edwardian rather than Elizabethan, and which the ritualists' own 1899 advocate conceded would, strictly interpreted, exclude incense.

⛔⛔ **RECOMMENDATION, AS A REPORT AND NOT AS AN ADJUDICATION: "In 1559 the Church of England outlawed incense" SHOULD NOT BE DEPLOYED IN THAT FORM.** It invites a reply — the retention proviso, the surviving censers — that is available in the ritualists' own 1899 submission, which the project already holds in full, and which would cost the argument credibility it does not need to spend.

⛔ **THE 1899 PROHIBITION WAS NOT RE-RESEARCHED**, per the brief. `Ritualist_Case_For_Incense_and_the_1899_Opinion.md` governs: Frere's and Darwell Stone's patristic concessions, and the Temple point that the Prayer Book gives the archbishops no power to punish, all stand there and are cross-referenced, not duplicated.

## 9. Honest assessment — what this survey will and will not bear

⭐ **WHAT IT WILL BEAR.**
- That **no** pre-1900 Protestant commentary in this set reads Malachi 1:11 as warranting physical incense. Verified, seventeen for seventeen.
- That a substantial minority of them (six to nine) **affirmatively exclude** a material reading, two of them stating it as a canon of interpretation with reasons attached (Keil & Delitzsch; Perowne), and one as a reductio (Ellicott).
- That **nobody in the set pairs a material *minchah* with material incense** — JD's "anybody at all" question, answered in the negative for this corpus. The nearest Protestant pairing is the inverse: Eucharistic offering **plus** incense-as-prayer (Barnes, following Irenaeus).
- That the same commentators read Psalm 141:2 and Revelation 5:8 figuratively, with substantial unanimity.

⚠️⚠️ **WHAT IT WILL NOT BEAR, AND THESE ARE THE THINGS TO KNOW BEFORE IT GOES ANYWHERE NEAR A LIVE EXCHANGE.**

1. ⛔⛔ **SILENCE IS NOT DENIAL — the same distinction as the RJ-silence-isn't-agreement rule, and the brief was right to demand it be flagged.** Eleven of the seventeen never address physical incense in churches at all. Reading their figurative exegesis as an affirmative denial of censing **is the identical error** the project refuses to make in the other direction. The count of *positions* is six to nine, not seventeen.
2. ⛔ **The seventeen are not seventeen independent judgments.** Wesley reproduces Poole. Others cite each other. It is a tradition, not a poll, and it should be characterised as a tradition.
3. ⛔⛔ **The modern control group is not clean, and one of its members is a liability.** Gaebelein and Constable read the verse as predicting **literal** incense in a future temple. Against a ritualist, they are quotable *for* literal incense by anyone willing to drop the dispensational frame. See §5.
4. ⛔⛔ **The Revelation 8:3 flank is open.** The Cambridge Bible — inside this very set — expressly distinguishes Rev 8 from Rev 5:8 on the identity question and uses the phrase "censes the holy things." The general form of the identity argument should be retired; the Rev 5:8-specific form survives.
5. ⛔ **Ellicott's conditional** leaves a future-material reading formally open, and a careful interlocutor will find it.
6. ⛔⛔ **"1559 outlawed incense" should not be deployed.** §7. The project's own file contains the counter-evidence.
7. ⚠️ **Aggregator dependence is heavy.** Thirteen of the seventeen rest on Tier 2 database text. ⭐ **This does not put the *readings* in doubt** — they are long, characteristic, and mutually corroborating across two independent aggregators — **but no page number in this file can be cited, and any verbatim deployment should be checked against a real edition first**, per `CLAUDE.md`'s standing rule that quotes are verified against the actual source before outward-facing use.

⭐⭐⭐ **THE BOTTOM LINE FOR THE RESERVE LIST.** The commentary survey's headline number **survives verification**. Its supporting architecture is **partly stronger** than claimed (Keil, Trapp, Simeon) and **partly weaker** (the silence/denial conflation, the modern group, Rev 8:3). ⛔ **The tactical memo's `Do not deploy until a verification pass runs` is discharged — but it should be replaced with a narrower instruction, not simply removed: deploy the 0-of-17 on Malachi 1:11; do not deploy the 1559 claim, the general Revelation identity argument, or the modern control group without its dispensational qualification.**
