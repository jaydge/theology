## 3. What diverged — five items, reported not smoothed over

⭐ **STRENGTHENING (2).**

1. **The symmetry canon is Keil & Delitzsch's as well as Perowne's.** The submitted table credited it to Perowne alone. K&D state it as a **rule of construction** in a footnote: the literal reading of *minchâh* is *"precluded by the parallel 'burning incense'."* **The survey's sharpest instrument is attested twice, in two traditions, twenty-four years apart — not once.**
2. **Trapp answers the entire ritualist proof-text cluster in one place, in 1660.** The table recorded only his rejection of Bellarmine. He in fact yokes **Mal 1:11 + Rev 5:8 + Rev 8:3-4 + Ps 141:2** and reads all four figuratively, attributing the reading to Irenaeus, Tertullian and Augustine. **The cluster is not merely unaddressed by the older Protestants; it is assembled and answered as a cluster.**

⚠️ **QUALIFYING (2).**

3. **Ellicott's spiritual reading carries an unreported conditional.** *"Unless we are to expect some future establishment of a universal offering of material sacrifices…"* He does not close the future-material door; he brackets it. The table presented him as flatly anti-literal. **A careful interlocutor will find this**, and it is the same door the millennialists in §4 walk through.
4. **The Apology of the Augsburg Confession concedes more than the table reported.** *"If any one would have this term embrace the ceremony, we readily concede it"* — subject to two conditions. The table rendered this as "ceremony only as thanksgiving," which is true but softer than the text. ⛔ Still nothing whatever for physical incense.

⛔ **METHODOLOGICAL (1).**

5. **The seventeen are not seventeen independent witnesses.** **Wesley's note is Poole's**, in substantially identical words — the *Explanatory Notes* being an avowed abridgement of earlier expositors. Others in the set cite each other. **It is a tradition, not a poll, and should be characterised as one.** At most **16** independent witnesses.

## 5. ⛔⛔ The Revelation 8:3 flank is open

The older Protestants read Ps 141:2 and Rev 5:8 figuratively with substantial unanimity. **Revelation 8:3-4 splits them**, and one of them helps the ritualist.

- **Angel = Christ, incense = his merit:** Gill, Poole, the Geneva notes. Nothing for a church to imitate.
- **Angel = created angel, incense *given* and *added* to the prayers:** Ellicott, JFB, Pulpit.
- ⚠️⚠️⚠️ **AND THE CAMBRIDGE BIBLE GOES FURTHER, IN THE RITUALIST'S DIRECTION.** At Rev 8:3 it says the prayers already lie on the altar and the angel — *"in modern liturgical phrase, 'censes the holy things'"* — and **expressly distinguishes this from Rev 5:8, *"where the incense is the prayers of the Saints, not something added to them."***

⛔⛔ **That is precisely the ritualist's Rev 8 argument, made by a pre-1900 Anglican commentary INSIDE THIS VERY SET — the same series as Perowne.** Cambridge draws no ceremonial conclusion from it. **But the submitted survey's claim that identity language works against "incense plus prayers as two elements" is TRUE OF REV 5:8 AND EXPRESSLY DENIED OF REV 8:3 by one of its own sources.**

⭐ **CONSEQUENCE: the general form of the identity argument must be RETIRED. Only the Rev 5:8-specific form survives.** Better found here than across the table.

⚠️ **Two smaller technical caveats, recorded because both slightly weaken forms the submitted survey asserted:** (a) Cambridge notes that at Rev 5:8, *strictly*, it is the **vials** and not the **odours** that are identified with the prayers — the identity argument is not a knock-down grammatical point; (b) Keil & Delitzsch observe that at Ps 141:2 the psalmist **purposely omits the כּ of comparison**, so *"the grammar is simile, not institution"* is a shade too quick. **Both readings still stand on sense and context; neither should be argued from the particle.**

## 6. The 1559 claim — ⛔ NOT SUPPORTED AS FRAMED

The tactical memo flagged this as the higher-risk claim. **It was right, and the complication is worse than it anticipated: some of the evidence runs the other way.**

1. ⛔ **No 1559 instrument examined NAMES incense** — not the Ornaments Rubric, not the Ornaments Proviso, not the Advertisements of 1566. Any prohibition is an **inference from a general rule about unauthorised ceremonies**. ⭐ **That is how JD stated the claim's *reasoning*; it is the verb "outlawed" that asserts a naming which did not happen.**
2. ⭐⭐ **The proviso is suspensive**, and the project already holds the proof: ornaments retained *"until it please the Queen to take other order for them"* (Sandys to Parker, 30 April 1559; quoted **twice** in the Westall submission).
3. ⛔⛔ **AND THE REPOSITORY HOLDS EVIDENCE POINTING THE OPPOSITE WAY** — the **Poole inventory of 1559** listing censers in a parish **certified as conforming** to the Prayer Book and Injunctions, and the **Bodmin indenture of 1566** committing a censer *"to be used and occupied."* A conforming parish holding censers is very hard to square with "1559 outlawed incense."
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **The strongest version of the exclusion argument is already in the repository and comes from the ritualists' own advocate — but it is EDWARDIAN, not Elizabethan.** Lacey concedes that Ridley's Injunction confining the minister to *"only the ceremonies and gestures appointed by the Book of Common Prayer, and none other"* would, *"strictly interpreted… exclude the use of Incense."* **That is the mechanism JD's claim describes, conceded by the other side, and worth far more than the 1559 framing.**
5. ⚠️ The 1899 Archbishops themselves conceded the Act *"was imperfectly obeyed."*

⛔⛔ **RECOMMENDATION, AS A REPORT AND NOT AN ADJUDICATION: do not deploy "In 1559 the Church of England outlawed incense."** It invites a reply available in the ritualists' own 1899 submission, which the project holds **in full**, and would cost credibility the argument does not need to spend.

## 8. ⭐⭐⭐ The thing JD should know before using any of this

**The brief asked explicitly for this flag, and it is the pass's most important methodological result.**

⛔⛔ **READING A COMMENTARY'S SILENCE ON PHYSICAL INCENSE AS AN AFFIRMATIVE DENIAL IS AN OVERREACH — AND IT IS THE IDENTICAL ERROR TO READING REV. JAMES'S SILENCE AS AGREEMENT, the distinction the last pass had to draw.**

The file therefore splits Bucket B in two:

- **B-explicit — 6 of 17** (9 at the widest defensible reading): affirmatively rejects a material reading, or refuses the inference to church ceremonial. **An argument.**
- **B-silent — 11 of 17**: reads the clause figuratively and **never addresses censing at all**. **Not an argument — an absence of one.**

⭐ **THE HONEST FORM, WHICH IS STILL STRONG:** *Of seventeen pre-1900 Protestant commentaries examined, none reads Malachi 1:11 as warranting physical incense; six to nine affirmatively rule out a material reading, several as a stated canon of interpretation; and not one pairs a material* minchah *with material incense.*

⛔ **THE FORM THAT SHOULD NOT BE DEPLOYED:** *"All seventeen deny that incense may be burned."* **Eleven of them were never asked the question.**

✅ **JD's "anybody at all" question, answered:** **no Protestant commentator in this corpus pairs a Eucharistic *minchah* with physical burning of incense.** The nearest Protestant pairing is the **inverse** — Eucharistic offering **plus** incense-as-prayer (Barnes, following Irenaeus).
