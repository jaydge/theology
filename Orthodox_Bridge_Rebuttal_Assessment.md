## 3. The fifteen, one by one

### 3.1 CA-1 — Source-weighting: bishops and councils over converts and suspect theologians ⭐ **NEW**

**What it argues.** *"The Orthodox approach is to give the greater weight to the witness of a bishop or a church council over that of a new convert or a theologian whose orthodoxy is in question."* Arnobius is discounted as a new convert who *"occasionally lapsed into unorthodoxy"* (sourced to the *New International Dictionary of the Christian Church*); Tertullian *"is not regarded as a church father"* so *"his writings need to be used judiciously"*; Origen is *"brilliant but erratic"* and not a Father.

**Repo cross-reference.** ⛔ **Nothing in the repo engages a witness-weighting rule of this kind.** `260835-47` grades the eight citations for accuracy and register but applies **no ecclesial-standing filter** at any point. **Genuinely new.**

**Verdict: NEW — and its force is limited, arithmetically, and it cuts both ways.**

- ⭐ **It does not reach the strongest citations.** Apply the filter at full strength and it removes Tertullian (##1, 2, 3) and, on a stretch, Lactantius (#8, whom Arakaki separately discounts by quoting the *Catholic Encyclopedia* on his *"almost utter ignorance of Scripture"*). **It leaves Justin ×2, Athenagoras and Barnabas standing — and `260835-47` §5 already ranks Athenagoras and Justin *1 Apol.* 13 as the two safest of the eight, with Tertullian's three among the caveated and the do-not-deploy.** The filter subtracts mostly from the part of the set the repo had already discounted on its own grounds.
- ⛔⛔ **And it is self-harming.** Discounting Tertullian discards `260835-47`'s **counter-evidence** as well as its evidence: *De Corona* 10 (Tertullian burning frankincense at home) and *Apology* 42 (Christians buying Sabaean incense in bulk for funerals) are the two passages a pro-incense advocate would least want removed. **Arakaki's own filter costs him more than it saves him** — and, as §5 shows, he had already lost both passages by other means.

**If raised in person.** Ask what the rule is doing rather than disputing it: *does the standing filter apply symmetrically — does it also set aside Tertullian burning frankincense in his own house?* ⭐ Lock the symmetry before naming the case, per `CLAUDE.md`'s funnel rule.

### 3.2 CA-2 — The context filter ✅✅ **ADDRESSED, AND CONCEDED**

**What it argues.** Three contexts must be distinguished: polemic against incense in **pagan** worship, against incense in **hypocritical Jewish** worship, and against incense in **Christian** worship — and Brattston, *"by indiscriminately invoking numerous citations without regard for their context,"* runs them together. Applied seriatim to Justin *1 Apol.* 13 (*"we are not atheists"* → the target is pagan sacrifice), Athenagoras *Plea* 13 (the same atheism charge), *Barnabas* 2 (hypocritical Old Testament worship), Lactantius 6.25 (a pagan source criticising pagan worship), Justin *2 Apol.* 5 (demons and idols), Eusebius *DE* 3.3 and 3.6 (pagan worship; magic), Tertullian *Apol.* 30 (pagan perfunctoriness), Arnobius 7.26 (*"**your** ceremonies"*).

**Repo cross-reference.** ⭐⭐⭐ **`260835-47` §3, reached independently and stated more sharply than Arakaki states it:**

> *"EVERY ONE OF THE SIX THEOLOGICAL ARGUMENTS IS ABOUT INCENSE AS A SACRIFICIAL OBLATION OFFERED* TO *GOD, AND EVERY ONE IS MADE WHILE REFUSING PAGAN OR JEWISH SACRIFICIAL CULT. NOT ONE OF THE EIGHT ADDRESSES INCENSE USED CEREMONIALLY OR HONORIFICALLY IN A CHRISTIAN ASSEMBLY."*

And `260835-49` Task 3 says the same of the article rather than of the citations: Brattston *"never argues for the move from 'condemned as pagan-style sacrificial oblation' to 'condemned in any Christian liturgical use whatsoever' — it treats the two as obviously the same claim."*

**Verdict: ADDRESSED — and the repo's version is the stronger one.** ⭐ `260835-47` frames it as a *contrast-class* finding (the contrast class in each case is a victim on an altar: blood, libations, burnt-offerings, a hecatomb, the fat of lambs), which is a more precise instrument than Arakaki's three-context taxonomy and is verified passage by passage rather than asserted.

⚠️⚠️ **ONE PLACE WHERE THE REPO IS AHEAD OF THE ARTICLE, AND IT IS THE ONLY PURCHASE AVAILABLE ON CA-2.** `260835-47` §2 records the distinction Arakaki collapses:

> *"Athenagoras's chapter is framed as a **rebuttal to the charge of atheism** and Tertullian's as a **rebuttal to the charge of disloyalty**. The apologetic setting is prudential; **the reason given inside it is not.**"*

⭐ **Arakaki argues from the setting to the reason.** *"The phrase 'we are not atheists' tells us that Justin is refuting the offering up of incense to the pagan gods"* — but the sentence Justin gives as his *reason* is not about pagans at all: God *"has no need of streams of blood and libations and incense,"* and the honour worthy of Him is **not to consume by fire what He made for our sustenance** but to use it for the needy. ⭐⭐ **That second ground is `260835-47` #4's *"the most transferable argument in the whole set: it is not merely 'God does not need it' but 'consuming it by fire is not what it is for.'"*** A polemical occasion does not confine a stated reason to that occasion.

⛔⛔ **BUT THE REPO ALSO RECORDS THE LIMIT OF THAT REPLY, AND IT MUST TRAVEL WITH IT** (`260835-47` §2): *"That does not convert their stated reasons into prudential ones — but it does mean none of the six is a liturgical directive, and none was written to a church about its own worship."*

**If raised in person.** ⭐ **Concede CA-2 immediately and without hedging** — it is the repo's own finding, conceding it costs nothing, and conceding it *first* is worth more than defending it. Then take the single available step: *granted the occasion was pagan; the reason Justin gives is about what God is and what fire is for. Does that reason stop at the temple door?* ⛔ One question, and stop there.

### 3.3 CA-3 — Barnabas and Isaiah condemn hypocrisy, not incense ✅ **ADDRESSED**

**What it argues.** *Barnabas* 2 quotes Isaiah 1 against **hypocritical** Old Testament worship, not against incense; *"It would be like a husband who is cheating on his wife bringing home an extra nice bouquet of roses."* The Old Testament worship *"while good… was limited and for that reason was superseded."*

**Repo cross-reference.** ⭐ **`260835-47` #6 already grades this citation **(B) OVERSTATED**, and on grounds Arakaki does not reach:** Barnabas's own list in his own voice is *"neither sacrifices, nor burnt-offerings, nor oblations"* — **incense is not in it**; the word occurs exactly once in the chapter and it is **inside the Isaiah quotation**; and the abolition clause is a separate verse (2.6) whose object is the whole Isaianic catalogue.

**Verdict: ADDRESSED — repo agrees, and got there by a better route.**

⚠️ **BUT ARAKAKI OMITS THE HALF OF BARNABAS THAT DOES NOT HELP HIM.** `260835-47` #6 records Barnabas's register as **divine non-need *plus supersession*** — *"the old cult is abolished so that the new law of Christ, 'without the yoke of necessity,' may have its own oblation"* — and calls it *"the only one of the eight that supplies a fulfilment-of-the-ceremonial-law argument."* ⭐ **Arakaki quotes the annulment clause in his own excerpt** (*"These things therefore He **annulled**, that the new law of our Lord Jesus Christ… might have its oblation not made by human hands"*) **and then reads the passage as being about hypocrisy only.** The words *"not made by human hands"* are in his own block quotation and go unaddressed.

⛔⛔ **AND THE SYMMETRY PROBLEM RUNS BACK AT JD, WHICH IS WHY THIS IS NOT A FREE HIT.** `260835-47` #6 also records: *"He spiritualises the incense; he does not repudiate the category… A ritualist can point out that Barnabas is comfortable with the imagery."* Barnabas's replacement is stated in sacrificial-odour language — *"a smell of sweet savour to the Lord is a heart that glorifies Him."*

**If raised in person.** *"Annulled… that the new law might have its oblation not made by human hands"* is the sentence to have ready — it is in the article's own excerpt. ⚠️ Expect the sweet-savour answer and concede it when it comes.

### 3.4 CA-4 — Eusebius, *Demonstratio Evangelica* 1.10 ⛔⛔ **NEW + NEEDS CHECKING — HIGHEST PRIORITY**

**What it argues.** This is **the article's single strongest positive citation** and Arakaki treats it as such, calling it *"apparently overlooked by Brattston"* and returning to it in the Conclusion. Eusebius, commenting on Malachi 1:11:

> *"**So, then, we sacrifice and offer incense**: On the one hand when we celebrate the Memorial of His great Sacrifice according to the Mysteries He delivered to us… while on the other we consecrate ourselves to Him alone…"*

Arakaki: *"Eusebius' concluding line… contradicts Brattston's argument."*

**Repo cross-reference.** ⛔⛔ **NONE. Eusebius does not appear anywhere in `260835-47`, `260835-48` or `260835-49`'s verified or spot-checked material.** A repo-wide sweep finds *Demonstratio Evangelica* nowhere; the two `Eusebius` hits in the corpus are to the *Church History* on an unrelated question (Lent dating, `RJ_Final_Question_List.md`). ⛔ **This is genuinely new to the project and is UNVERIFIED.**

**Verdict: NEEDS CHECKING — and it is the one item on this list JD is least prepared for.**

⚠️ **THREE THINGS THAT CAN BE SAID WITHOUT OPENING THE TEXT, ALL OF THEM PROVISIONAL AND NONE OF THEM A SUBSTITUTE FOR CHECKING IT.**

1. ⚠️ **The article's own quotation appears to gloss its key word metaphorically, in the same paragraph.** Two sentences before *"So, then, we sacrifice and offer incense,"* Arakaki's own excerpt has Eusebius writing *"we offer the incense of the prophet, in every place bringing to Him the sweet-smelling fruit of the sincere Word of God, **offering it in our prayers to Him**,"* and citing Psalm 141:2. ⛔ **And Arakaki's own earlier quotation of *DE* 1.6 has Eusebius reading Malachi's incense clause as *"the **incense of prayer**"* explicitly** — a reading Arakaki accepts on the page (*"That incense is a symbol of our prayers to God is something an Orthodox Christian would readily agree with"*). **Whether *"we sacrifice and offer incense"* is literal or is the same metaphor continued is precisely the question, and the article does not argue for the literal reading; it asserts it.** ⏳ **This must be checked in the full chapter, not settled from the article's excerpt.**
2. ⛔ **Eusebius is not ante-Nicene in the useful sense, and the article concedes it in passing** — it introduces him as *"Rounding out Brattston's **fourth century** sources."* `260835-47` #8 already registers this exact defect against Lactantius: *"If the argument is 'the Fathers of the persecuted Church spoke against incense,' he is the weakest of the eight to lean on."* ⭐⭐ **The point applies with more force to Eusebius, who wrote the *Demonstratio* after Constantine's victory** — but ⚠️ it also cuts the way `260835-47` #8 notes it cuts: **a witness writing as the pressure lifts is exactly the witness whose testimony a prudential-abstention theory would predict.** ⛔ Do not treat the date as a refutation; it is a qualification.
3. ⚠️ **The Malachi 1:11 connection is not new to the project even though the Eusebius text is.** `Ritualist_Case` §2g records Frere's own report that in the patristic handling of Malachi i. 11 *"the allusion to Incense is either passed over in silence or explained as referring to prayer."* ⭐⭐ **Frere surveyed this literature for the pro-incense side and did not produce *DE* 1.10 as a counter-instance.** ⛔ That is not proof it fails — Frere's Appendix A is a survey, not an exhaustive concordance, and `Frere_Appendix_A_Translated.md` records his nine quoted passages, Eusebius among them nowhere. **But it is a real datum: the ritualists' own senior liturgist, working the same verse, reported the patristic result as prayer-or-silence.**

⏳⏳ **OWED, AND NOT DONE HERE: read *Demonstratio Evangelica* Book 1, ch. 10 in full at Tier 1** (Ferrar's translation is at `tertullian.org/fathers/eusebius_de_03_book1.htm`, the host the article itself links) **and determine whether the "sacrifice and offer incense" sentence is literal or is the prayer-metaphor continued.** ⛔ **Until that is done, no verdict on CA-4 is available in either direction, and this document does not supply one.**

**If raised in person.** ⛔ **Do not contest it unread.** The honest position is *"I haven't read that chapter — I've read the 1.6 passage where he glosses Malachi's incense as prayer, and I'd want to see whether 1.10 is the same figure or a different claim."* ⭐ That is a stronger answer than a guess, and it is true.

### 3.5 CA-5 — Arguing from silence ✅ **ADDRESSED — and this is the article's weakest link**

**What it argues.** Of Brattston's Pliny / Justin *1 Apol.* 61, 65–67 / *Didache* section: *"arguing from silence is very problematic."* Repeated against Hippolytus's *Commentary on Canticles*: *"As noted earlier, arguing from silence is not a strong argument."* And in the Conclusion: *"Brattston argues from silence, a highly dubious form of argumentation."*

**Repo cross-reference.** ⭐⭐⭐ **`260835-49` Task 3 grades this Brattston's *strongest single point*, and on a structural ground Arakaki never engages:**

> *"the argument-from-silence section (Didache, Pliny, Justin's summary in* 1 Apology *61 and 65–67, and especially Hippolytus's* Apostolic Tradition, *which itemizes exorcism-breath, oil, water, milk and honey, specific fruits, flowers, cheese and olives but never incense) is a genuinely different and independently defensible line of argument — **silence in an exhaustive ritual inventory carries real evidentiary weight**."*

**Verdict: ADDRESSED — and Arakaki answers a generic fallacy label instead of the actual argument.** ⛔ **He never mentions the *Apostolic Tradition*'s itemised inventory at all.** He answers *"argument from silence"* as a category. The category objection is sound against bare absence; it is much weaker against a list that names cheese and olives and omits incense.

⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE REASON THIS IS SAFE FOR JD TO PRESS IS THAT THE REPO HAS ALREADY GRANTED THE PRINCIPLE — RUNNING THE OTHER WAY, AGAINST JD'S OWN SIDE.** `Ritualist_Case` §7d(1)(a) rates as a *genuine strengthening* of the ritualist case the fact that the Edwardine Articles of c. 1549–50 and Ridley's Visitation Articles of 1550 *"enumerate the forbidden 'popish' acts by name — some twenty of them, including sacring bells, elevation, lights on the Lord's board, holy water, palms, ashes, candles, creeping to the cross, oil, chrism and altars — and **incense is in none of the lists**,"* and calls that *"not bare silence; it is silence inside what is on its face an exhaustive enumeration of the very category, drawn up by the men most hostile to what it enumerates."*

⭐⭐ **So the project already holds that itemised silence carries weight, and holds it in a place where it *costs* JD something.** ⛔ **That is exactly why he can press it here without inconsistency — and it is also why he cannot press the generic version.** The distinction is between bare silence (weak, both directions) and enumerated silence (strong, both directions), and JD is committed to it symmetrically.

⚠️ **ONE LIMIT, RECORDED SO IT IS NOT OVERSTATED:** `260835-49` explicitly did **not** verify the *Didache*, Pliny *Ep.* 10.96, Justin *1 Apol.* 61/65–67, or the *Apostolic Tradition* against primary text. ⛔ **The inventory claim is reported from Brattston, not independently checked.**

**If raised in person.** ⭐ **Lock the principle before naming the case.** *Does an itemised list that names twenty things and omits the twenty-first tell us anything?* — and note that JD is committed to the answer in both directions, including where it helps the ritualists on the Edwardine Articles. ⛔ Do not name Hippolytus until the rule is agreed.

## 5. ⚠️⚠️ BRIEF ITEM 5 — the Tertullian counter-evidence: does the article address it, omit it, or misuse it?

⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANSWER IS THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT THE BRIEF ANTICIPATED, AND IT IS THE MOST SURPRISING FINDING OF THE PASS.** The brief asked whether the article uses the domestic-frankincense and funerary-purchase passages **as evidence FOR its position**, requiring a clear flag because `260835-47`/`48` already determined these do not establish liturgical use. ⛔⛔ **It does neither. It omits one and misreads the other against its own interest.**

### 5.1 *De Corona* 10 — Tertullian burning frankincense in his own house: ⛔⛔ **ABSENT ENTIRELY**

**Finding.** ⛔ **The article never cites *De Corona* at all** — not chapter 10, not chapter 11. Its Tertullian material is *Apology* 42 and *Apology* 30, and nothing else.

⭐⭐⭐ **THE SIGNIFICANCE, STATED PLAINLY: THE SINGLE PASSAGE `260835-47` CALLS "THE PASS'S SHARPEST WARNING" IS MISSING FROM THE ONE ARTICLE THAT WOULD HAVE PROFITED MOST FROM IT.** `260835-47` #1 records that citing *De Corona* 10 *"hands the opponent a gift"* — Tertullian saying, in the first person, *"if the smell of some place or other offends me, I burn the Arabian product myself,"* and arguing that the substances are *"creatures of God without impurity"* whose defilement lies in *"the ministries to which in their use they are devoted."* `260835-48` §3 calls it *"a **permission**, stated in Tertullian's own first person,"* which *"undercuts rather than supports any argument that the early Fathers regarded incense as inherently idolatrous."*

⛔⛔⛔ **AND BRATTSTON CITES IT AND ARAKAKI DOES NOT.** `260835-49` Task 1 records Brattston's footnote 11 — *"In De corona he conceded that animals can be killed and incense burned for secular purposes but not for worship"* — and grades the handling **honest**: *"an accurate summary of what chapter 10 actually says… the substance is fairly reported, and lands in the same place the primary-text verification did."*

⭐ **So on this passage the anti-incense article discloses the pro-incense evidence and the pro-incense rebuttal does not.**

⚠️ **THE SAME IS TRUE OF *DE IDOLOLATRIA* 11, WHICH IS THE STRONGER PASSAGE.** `260835-48` §5c calls it *"the single most systematic statement on incense anywhere in the Tertullian material this project has now examined"* — Tertullian forbidding the frankincense-seller's trade while naming **medicinal** and **funerary** use as licit in the same sentence. `260835-49` Task 1 records that **Brattston quotes its actual carve-out language directly** (footnote 12) and calls the handling *"better than adequate."* ⛔ **Arakaki does not mention *De Idololatria* anywhere.**

### 5.2 *Apology* 42 — the funerary purchase: ⛔⛔⛔ **USED, AND MISREAD AGAINST ITS OWN INTEREST**

**What the article says, in full:**

> *"Brattston's first citation is Tertullian's* Apology *42 in which he **criticizes the burial custom of using frankincense to cover up the smell of the dead body**."*

That is the article's **entire** treatment. It then moves to *Apology* 30 without further comment.

⛔⛔⛔ **THAT IS NOT WHAT THE CHAPTER SAYS, AND THE REPO HAS THE TEXT DOUBLE-ATTESTED.** `260835-48` §5, from New Advent and CCEL, word-for-word identical on both hosts:

> *"We **certainly buy no frankincense**. If the Arabias complain of this, let the Sabæans be well assured that their more precious and costly merchandise **is expended as largely in the burying of Christians** as in the fumigating of the gods."*

`260835-48` §5: *"The incense line is commercial bookkeeping, not theology: temple revenue is down… but the Sabaean incense-traders lose nothing, because Christians spend just as much buying incense for funerals as pagans spend burning it to their gods."* ⛔ **Tertullian is not criticising the burial custom. He is citing it, approvingly and almost with pride** (`260835-48`: *"worth mentioning almost with pride ('let the Sabæans be well assured')"*), **as proof that Christians are not economically useless.**

⭐⭐⭐ **THE FLAG THE BRIEF ASKED FOR, INVERTED.** `260835-47` §3 calls *Apology* 42 *"the citation that most clearly cuts against the argument it is deployed for"* — meaning against **Brattston's**. ⛔⛔ **But Arakaki, defending incense, converts a passage in which Christians buy Sabaean frankincense in bulk without embarrassment into a passage in which Tertullian *criticises* a burial custom.** **He turns his own best available evidence into a condemnation.**

⚠️⚠️ **AND HIS ERROR IS THE SAME SHAPE AS BRATTSTON'S, BUT LARGER.** `260835-49` Task 1 faults Brattston's footnote 9 for paraphrasing the passage as Christians spending on burial *"instead of"* incense — *"a reader of the article would come away thinking Christians substituted burial expenses for incense expenses, not that the burial expenses **were** incense expenses."* ⛔ **Brattston obscures the point. Arakaki reverses its valence.**

### 5.3 ⛔⛔ The guard that must travel with all of this

⛔⛔⛔ **THE REPO'S OWN FINDING BINDS, AND JD MUST NOT OVERCLAIM IN THE OTHER DIRECTION.** `260835-48` §5 is explicit:

> *"The distinction drawn in the text is between **cultic/idolatrous use** ('fumigating of the gods') and **funerary use** ('the burying of Christians'). Christian liturgical incense — used in Christian worship, directed to God — is a **third category this passage never raises at all**. It cannot be read as evidence either for or against that third category."*

⭐ **So the correct account of the two passages is: they establish domestic and funerary Christian use of incense, without embarrassment, in the early third century; they are silent on assembly.** ⛔ **They are not evidence that the Fathers approved liturgical incense, and JD must not present them as though they were — that would be the mirror image of the error he is noting in Arakaki.**

⚠️ **A THIRD PASSAGE, RECORDED SO THE SET IS COMPLETE AND NOT CHERRY-PICKED.** `260835-48` §5b records *De Idololatria* 9 — the Magi's frankincense offered to the infant Christ, framed by Tertullian as *"the close of worldly sacrifice and glory, which Christ was about to do away."* ⛔⛔ **`260835-48` calls it *"genuinely double-edged"* and rules: *"Do not deploy this one-sided."*** ⭐ **Arakaki does not use it. A commenter on the article does** (Fr. John Morris, first comment: *"the fact that the Magi offered incense to the infant Christ would be sufficient proof"*) — ⚠️ recorded as an observation about where the argument tends to go in conversation, **not** as part of the article's case and **not** as anything attributable to its author.

**If raised in person.** ⭐⭐ **If Rev. James produces *Apology* 42 or *De Corona* 10 for incense, the answer is neither to contest them nor to grant more than they say:** *those establish that Christians bought and burned incense domestically and at funerals without any sense of impropriety — which I accept, and which is more than Brattston's framing allows. What neither one touches is the assembly.* ⛔ **That is `260835-48` §5's finding, and it is the same finding whichever side produces the passage.**

## 6. ⚠️⚠️ BRIEF ITEM 6 — Frere's imperial-cult explanation and the register finding

⛔⛔⛔ **THE ARTICLE DOES NOT INVOKE THE IMPERIAL-CULT EXPLANATION. IT DOES NOT INVOKE ANY PRUDENTIAL EXPLANATION. THE WORDS AND THE MOVE ARE BOTH ABSENT.**

⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE REASON IS STRUCTURAL, NOT ACCIDENTAL: ARAKAKI HAS NO ABSTENTION TO EXPLAIN.** The prudential argument — *the Fathers avoided incense because the imperial cult made it read as apostasy, so their abstention says nothing about incense in principle* — presupposes that there **was** an ante-Nicene abstention. ⛔ **Arakaki's whole case (CA-2) is that there was not: the Fathers were only ever condemning pagan incense, so nothing needs explaining away.** He then argues affirmatively (CA-4, CA-6, CA-7) that the ante-Nicene Church **did** use incense. **A man arguing that they used it has no use for a theory of why they didn't.**

### 6.1 ⭐⭐⭐ What this means for the register finding, stated plainly

⛔⛔ **THE `260835-47` REGISTER FINDING — SIX OF EIGHT GIVE A THEOLOGICAL REASON, ONE PRUDENTIAL, ONE DESCRIPTIVE — DOES NOT ENGAGE THIS ARTICLE, AND DEPLOYING IT AGAINST THIS ARTICLE WOULD BE A CATEGORY ERROR.**

The register finding is an answer to a specific escape route. `260835-47` §2 states its target exactly: *"The brief raised this against a specific counter-argument RJ may make: that ante-Nicene abstention was **purely circumstantial** — the imperial cult made incense look like emperor-worship — and therefore says nothing about incense in principle."*

⭐ **That escape says: they abstained, but for the wrong kind of reason.**
⭐ **Arakaki's escape says: they never abstained; you have misread the addressee.**

⛔⛔ **These are different moves and the register finding answers only the first.** Showing that Justin, Athenagoras, Barnabas, Lactantius and Tertullian *Apol.* 30 give **theological** rather than prudential reasons establishes that their refusal was principled. **It does nothing to establish that their refusal was directed at Christian ceremonial rather than at pagan sacrifice — which is the only thing Arakaki disputes.** ⭐⭐ **Answering him with the register count would be answering a question he did not ask.**

### 6.2 ⚠️ What DOES engage Arakaki — and the repo already recorded it, against itself

⭐⭐ **`260835-47` §2's own qualification is the honest engagement, and it was written before this article was read:**

> *"⚠️ **ONE HONEST QUALIFICATION ON THIS RESULT, BECAUSE IT NARROWS WHAT THE SIX ESTABLISH.** Athenagoras's chapter is framed as a **rebuttal to the charge of atheism** and Tertullian's as a **rebuttal to the charge of disloyalty**. The apologetic setting is prudential; **the reason given inside it is not.** … ⛔ That does not convert their stated reasons into prudential ones — but it does mean **none of the six is a liturgical directive, and none was written to a church about its own worship.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **That last clause is Arakaki's entire thesis, conceded in advance by the project's own verification pass.** The only thing the repo holds back is the middle sentence — that a polemical *occasion* does not confine a stated *reason* to that occasion. ⭐ **That is the whole of the available reply to CA-2, and it is a narrow one.** See §3.2.

### 6.3 ⭐⭐ The prudential argument is still live — but it comes from Frere, not from this article, and it costs the pro-incense side more than it pays

⛔ **If Rev. James reaches for the imperial-cult explanation, he is reaching past Arakaki to Frere**, and the register finding **does** answer that reach. But `Ritualist_Case` §2g and §7d(2)(f) record what comes attached to it in the 1899 submission itself:

- Frere concedes *"the liturgical use of Incense is **not attested during the first three centuries**"* (Appendix A, printed p. 47).
- He surveys Athenagoras, Tertullian ×3, Clement ×2, Origen ×2, Arnobius, Lactantius and Augustine and reads them as **positively disproving** liturgical use: *"such passages as these can only be taken as **disproving (so far as they go) the liturgical use of Incense**."*
- ⛔ He **declines** the argument-from-silence escape that was available to him, stating it and then refusing to lean on it.
- ⭐ **His explanation has a second limb that is rarely quoted and is more damaging than the imperial cult:** *"the Biblical authority for incense, so far as it was drawn from Levitical ceremonial, **would have told against the use of incense rather than for it**."*
- He concedes that ceremonial traces are scarce even after Constantine, that liturgical-book evidence begins c. 700–800, and that altar-censing is *"one of the later forms."*
- ⭐⭐ **And he closes by declaring the whole patristic question legally irrelevant:** *"whatever history lies behind, it is this only that is of practical importance and legal value"* — the state of use in 1548–9.

⭐⭐⭐ **SO THE REGISTER FINDING HAS A TARGET, AND IT IS NOT THIS ARTICLE.** Against Frere's prudential explanation it is a real and specific answer: six of the eight give a stated theological cause, not a circumstantial one, and `260835-47` #8 adds the sharpest form of it — **Lactantius states a theological objection as the persecution ends, which is the moment a prudential theory predicts the objection should relax.**

⛔⛔ **BUT `Ritualist_Case` §2g's warning governs the deployment and is restated here rather than assumed carried:** *"Anyone who 'refutes' the ritualist case by showing the primitive Church did not use incense has refuted a claim the ritualists' own expert made first, in their own submission."* ⭐ **Producing Frere's concession is not a refutation of anything; it is a way of asking what the tradition claim rests on once the first three centuries are off the table.**
