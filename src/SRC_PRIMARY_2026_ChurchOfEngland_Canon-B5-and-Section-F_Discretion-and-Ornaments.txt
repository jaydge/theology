A. CANON B 5 — "Of the discretion of ministers in conduct of public
   prayer" — COMPLETE
======================================================================

"1. The minister who is to conduct the service may in his discretion
make and use variations which are not of substantial importance in any
form of service authorized by Canon B 1 according to particular
circumstances.

2. The minister having the cure of souls may on occasions for which no
provision is made in The Book of Common Prayer or by the General Synod
under Canon B 2 or by the Convocations, archbishops, or Ordinary under
Canon B 4 use forms of service considered suitable by him for those
occasions and may permit another minister to use the said forms of
service.

3. All variations in forms of service and all forms of service used
under this Canon shall be reverent and seemly and shall be neither
contrary to, nor indicative of any departure from, the doctrine of the
Church of England in any essential matter.

4. If any question is raised concerning the observance of the
provisions of this Canon it may be referred to the bishop in order that
he may give such pastoral guidance, advice or directions as he may
think fit, but such reference shall be without prejudice to the matter
in question being made the subject matter of proceedings under the
Ecclesiastical Jurisdiction Measure 1963.

5. In this Canon the expression 'form of service' has the same meaning
as in Canon B 1.

Note: The forms of service which have been approved by the Archbishops
or commended by the House of Bishops as being suitable for use by
ministers in exercise of their discretion under Canons B 4 or B 5
respectively are detailed [link to the supplementary material at C
below]."

Related, from Canon B 1 §2, same page:
"Every minister shall use only the forms of service authorized by this
Canon, except so far as he may exercise the discretion permitted by
Canon B 5. It is the minister's responsibility to have a good
understanding of the forms of service used and he shall endeavour to
ensure that the worship offered glorifies God and edifies the people."

FINDING ON A — IS THE DISCRETION BOUNDED? YES, ON ITS FACE, FOUR WAYS.
  1. By degree: §1 reaches only variations "which are not of
     substantial importance."
  2. By occasion: §2 reaches only "occasions for which no provision is
     made" in the BCP or by Synod, Convocations, archbishops or
     Ordinary. It is a gap-filling power, not a general one.
  3. By content: §3, "reverent and seemly", and "neither contrary to,
     nor indicative of any departure from, the doctrine of the Church
     of England in any essential matter."
  4. By reviewability: §4, referable to the bishop for "pastoral
     guidance, advice or directions", and expressly without prejudice
     to proceedings under the Ecclesiastical Jurisdiction Measure 1963.
Canon B 5 does NOT name incense. Its §1/§2/§3/§4 track ss.5/6/7(1)/7(2)
of the 1965 Measure almost verbatim (see the companion capture,
SRC_PRIMARY_1964-1974_...).
CLASSIFICATION: PERMISSION BY SILENCE.
⛔ Two readings are live in the literature and BOTH must be carried:
   (a) that the repeal of the 1662 statutory frame ended the Victorian
       ceremonial case law, so ceremonies not provided for are now
       prima facie permitted; and
   (b) Bursell's contrary reading, that ceremonies remain regulated by
       Canons B 1 - B 5, on which a ceremony outside the authorized
       forms is no more lawful now than before. Do not report the
       modern English position as settled.

   list of subject-headings, F 1 to F 18
======================================================================

F 3  Of the communion plate
F 4  Of the communion linen
F 5  Of surplices for the minister
F 6  Of the reading desks and pulpit
F 7  Of seats in church
F 8  Of church bells
F 9  Of the Bible and The Book of Common Prayer for the use of the
       minister
F 10 Of the alms box
F 11 Of the register books and their custody
F 12 Of the register book of services
F 13 Of the care and repair of churches
F 14 Of the provision of things appertaining to churches
F 15 Of churches not to be profaned
F 16 Of plays, concerts, and exhibitions of films and pictures in
       churches
F 17 Of keeping a record of the property of churches
F 18 Of the survey of churches

The named objects across the whole section are: a font with a cover; a
table of wood, stone or other suitable material, with a covering of
silk or other decent stuff and a fair white linen cloth; a chalice, a
paten or other vessel, a basin for alms, a cruet or flagon; fair white
linen cloths; surplices; desks and a pulpit; seats; at least one bell;
a Bible including the Apocrypha, a Book of Common Prayer, a pulpit
Bible, a service book, a cushion or desk; an alms box; register books.

The one place the section uses the word "ornaments" is not a list of
them but a control on changing them — F 13 §3: "It shall be the duty of
the minister and churchwardens, if any alterations, additions,
removals, or repairs are proposed to be made in the fabric, ornaments,
or furniture of the church, to obtain the faculty or licence of the
Ordinary before proceeding to execute the same." F 18 uses the same
triad for the archdeacon's triennial survey.

FINDING ON B: Section F is the Church of England's own exhaustive
statement of what a church must provide and how its ornaments are
controlled. Incense, a censer and a thurible appear NOWHERE in it —
neither commanded, nor permitted, nor forbidden. This is a negative
over a complete and closed list, not an unbounded search, which is what
gives it weight.
CLASSIFICATION: PERMISSION BY SILENCE.
NOTE FOR §4 (conditions): the one condition Section F would impose on
introducing a censer as a permanent church ornament is F 13 §3 — the
faculty or licence of the Ordinary for an addition to the ornaments of
the church. That is a condition on the OBJECT and its introduction into
the fabric, not on the ceremonial act. Do not overstate it as a
requirement of the bishop's consent to censing.

C. THE AUTHORIZED / COMMENDED DISTINCTION — and what it does to the one
   Common Worship rubric that names incense
======================================================================

From the supplementary material to the Canons, the page Canon B 5's own
Note points to, under the heading "Approved and Commended forms of
service under Canons B 2, B 4 and B 5":

Under "Authorized Services" (list of 31 items, "As at 1 January 2012"),
the ONLY item listed under the sub-heading "Published in Common
Worship: Daily Prayer (Fourth Impression 2010)" is:
  "26. The Calendar"
and the list closes: "The above are all authorized for use until
further resolution of the Synod."

Under the next heading:
  "Commended services and resources
   (Material commended by the House of Bishops as being suitable for
   use by ministers in exercise of their discretion under Canon B 5)
   (see Canon B 5, [link])
   As at 1 January 2012"
and within that list, under "Services which comply with the provisions
of a Service of the Word":
  "6. Services contained in Common Worship: Daily Prayer"

FINDING ON C — THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING DISTINCTION OF §2.
The single Common Worship rubric that names incense permissively is in
Common Worship: Daily Prayer, at The Blessing of the Light: "Verses
from Psalm 141 - A Song of the Evening Sacrifice may be said and
incense may be burned." (captured by the companion pass at
SRC_PRIMARY_1928-2000_ChurchOfEngland_ServiceBooks_Incense-Search.txt.)
By the Church of England's own schedule, the SERVICES in Common
Worship: Daily Prayer are COMMENDED by the House of Bishops, not
AUTHORIZED by the General Synod. Only the Calendar out of that volume
is authorized.
So the rubric is not a Synod grant. It is a sentence inside material
the House of Bishops commends as suitable for a minister to use under
his own Canon B 5 discretion — and Canon B 5, as A above shows, never
names incense. The permission runs through B 5; the rubric describes
what may happen inside a service used under it.
CLASSIFICATION: MENTION WITHOUT GRANT — but recorded as the STRONGEST
CANDIDATE anywhere found in the Church of England for a positive
permission by name, and the one an opponent is most likely to reach
for. The contrary reading, which must be kept live: a House of Bishops
commendation is an act of a body of the church, and material it
commends which says "incense may be burned" is at least an official
statement that burning incense is a thing a minister may lawfully do.
That reading does not make it a grant, but it is not frivolous, and
the honest position is that the rubric is permissive language inside a
non-authorized text.
