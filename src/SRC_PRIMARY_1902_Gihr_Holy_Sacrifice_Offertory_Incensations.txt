(1) BLESSING OF THE INCENSE AT THE OFFERTORY — p. 535 (leaf 539)

Latin as printed:
    "Per intercessionem beati Michaelis Archangeli stantis a dextris altaris
    incensi, et omnium electorum suorum, incensum istud dignetur Dominus
    bene✠dicere, et in odorem suavitatis accipere. Per Christum Dominum
    nostrum. Amen."
    [OCR reads "Archangel! stands a dextris" for "Archangeli stantis a dextris";
     NO comma after "Archangeli" in this printing; "bene✠dicere" appears as
     "benefdicere", the OCR's rendering of the cross.]

English as printed:
    "By the intercession of blessed Michael the Archangel, standing at the right
    hand of the Altar of Incense, and of all His elect, may the Lord vouchsafe
    to bless✠ this incense, and receive it as an odor of sweetness. Through
    Christ our Lord. Amen."
    [OCR gives "blesst" for "bless✠" and "au" for "an"; these two are artifacts.]

Gihr's footnote 2, same page:
    "On account of the addition *stantis a dextris altaris incensi*, which in
    St. Luke, 1, 11—19 is stated literally of the Archangel Gabriel, there is to
    be found in many of the more ancient Missals the name of Gabriel instead of
    Michael; therefore, many liturgists would have this prayer thus changed. But
    they are mistaken. For well established reasons the Church adheres to the
    name of Michael… (cf. S. R. C. 25. Sept. 1705)."

(2) CENSING THE OBLATA — p. 536 (leaf 540)

Latin as printed:
    "Incensum istud, a te benedictum, ascendat ad te, Domine: et descendat
    super nos misericordia tua."
    [Note the comma after "istud", absent from most modern citations.
     OCR of the first word is "Iiicensurn".]

English as printed:
    "May this incense which Thou hast blessed, O Lord, ascend to Thee, and may
    Thy mercy descend upon us."
    [Doubly confirmed: this English appears both in the parallel column and, in
     quotation marks, in Gihr's running commentary on the same page.]

Gihr, p. 373 (leaf 377) n. 3, on the Missal/Breviary parallel:
    "*Incensum istud a te benedictum, ascendat ad te, Domine: et descendat super
    nos misericordia tua* (Miss. Rom.). — *Vespertina oratio ascendat ad te,
    Domine — et descendat super nos misericordia tua* (Breviar. Roman.)."

(3) CENSING THE ALTAR, Ps 140:2-4 — pp. 537-538 (leaves 541-542)

Latin as printed (breaking across the page turn):
    "Dirigatur, Domine, oratio mea, sicut incensum, in conspectu tuo: elevatio
    manuum mearum sacrificium vespertinum. Pone, Domine, custodiam ori meo, et
    ostium circumstantiae labiis meis: ut non declinet cor meum…"
    [Note the comma after "incensum". The continuation "in verba malitiae, ad
     excusandas excusationes in peccatis" falls on p. 538 and could NOT be
     captured verbatim from the OCR index — treat that clause as UNVERIFIED in
     Gihr's printing.]

English as printed:
    "Let my prayer, O Lord, be directed as incense in Thy sight: and the lifting
    up of my hands as the evening sacrifice. Set a watch, O Lord, before my
    mouth, and a door round about my lips. That my heart may not incline to evil
    words, to make excuses…"
    [The words on p. 538 recovered separately read "to evil words, to make
     excuses". The closing "in sins." was NOT captured verbatim — do not present
     the last two words as verified Gihr.]

Gihr's footnote (p. 538, quoting Dionysius the Carthusian) records a variant:
    "'Non declines' (Missale: 'ut non declinet')".

(4) RETURNING THE THURIBLE — p. 540 (leaf 544)

Gihr gives this in running text, not in a column:
    "When the priest returns the censer to the deacon, he says: *Accendat in
    nobis Dominus ignem sui amoris, et flammam aeternae caritatis. Amen.* —
    'May the Lord enkindle within us the fire of His love and the flame of
    eternal charity. Amen.'"

(5) "AB ILLO BENEDICARIS" — p. 376 (leaf 380)

Gihr treats this at the FIRST incensing at High Mass (after the prayers at the
foot of the altar), where the formula is the ordinary one:

    "The celebrant places incense three times on the glowing coals, while
    saying: *Ab illo benedicaris — in cujus honore — cremaberis. — Amen.* —
    'Be thou blessed by Him in whose honor thou wilt be consumed. Amen.' Only
    after these words does he make the sign of the Cross over the burning grains
    of incense."
    [The em-dashes mark the three spoonfuls.]

Gihr's footnote 1, same page, on the wording variants:
    "In former editions of the Ceremon. Episcop. we read *in honorem* (l. 1,
    c. 23, n. 1); but the new editio typica (Ratisbonae 1886) has *in honore*.
    The original and correct mode of reading is without doubt *in cujus honore*.
    … The Ord. Rom. XIV, c. 71, has the formula: *Ab ipso sanctificeris, in
    cujus honore cremaberis.*"

(6) "SUPPLICES TE ROGAMUS" — Roman Canon. Latin at scan leaf 653; English at
    scan leaf 653; commentary at printed p. 667. (Leaf numbers are scan leaves,
    not printed pages, for this item.)

Latin as Gihr prints it:
    "Supplices te rogamus, omnipotens Deus: jube haec perferri per manus sancti
    Angeli tui in sublime altare tuum, in conspectu divinae majestatis tuae: ut
    quotquot ex hac altaris participatione sacrosanctum Filii tui Cor✠pus et
    San✠guinem sumpserimus, omni benedictione coelesti et gratia repleamur. Per
    eundem Christum Dominum nostrum. Amen."

English as printed:
    "3. We humbly beseech Thee, Almighty God, command these to be carried by the
    hands of Thy holy Angel to Thine Altar on high, in the presence of Thy
    divine Majesty, that as many of us as shall, by partaking at this Altar,
    receive the most sacred Body✝ and Blood✝ of Thy Son, may be filled with all
    heavenly blessing and grace. Through [the same Christ our Lord. Amen.]"
    [The OCR snippet breaks after "Through"; Gihr's commentary at leaf 673
     confirms "the petition concludes with the words: 'Through the same Christ
     our Lord. Amen.'"]
