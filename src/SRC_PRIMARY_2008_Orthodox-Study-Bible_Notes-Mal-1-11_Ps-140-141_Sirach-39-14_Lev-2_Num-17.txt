--- BEGIN CAPTURE 1 — note at MALACHI 1:11, printed p. 1054 ---

1:11 A remarkable and clear prophecy that the Gentiles, in their worship of God,
in every place or church will burn incense before Him—as Israel had done. To this
day, incense is offered in nearly every service of the Orthodox Church.

--- END CAPTURE 1 ---

--- BEGIN CAPTURE 2 — note at PSALMS 140; 141 (LXX numbering) ---

140; 141 Ps 140 and 141 are the psalms of the evening incense. These two psalms
are both sung at Vespers. Ps 141:2 was sung in Israel, and now in the Church,
during the offering of incense to God. As Rev 5:8 teaches, incense is the visible
sign of the prayers of all God's people. The prophet Malachi foretold its
continued use in every place, including in the offering of worship by the
Gentiles (Mal 1:11). Incense was brought to Christ as a gift at His birth, and it
has been used in the worship of the Church from her very beginning.

--- END CAPTURE 2 ---

--- BEGIN CAPTURE 4 — notes at LEVITICUS 2 (figurative register) ---

[at 2:1] Frankincense was also added to the various grain offerings. It typified
the prayers of the Church. In Vespers, the Church sings, "Let my prayer arise in
Your sight as incense." Co-laboring with God must be accompanied by prayer.

[at 2:13] Salt, a symbol of an indissoluble covenant, was added to this offering.
The oil symbolized the Holy Spirit, and incense was a symbol of prayer.

READING RECORDED (for the analysis document, not part of the source):
  ⛔⛔ CAPTURE 1 IS THE COUNTEREXAMPLE THIS PASS WAS SENT TO LOOK FOR.
     The Orthodox Study Bible reads the incense clause of Mal 1:11 LITERALLY,
     locates its fulfilment in the CHURCH AGE and in the CHURCH's own worship,
     and says so in as many words. It does NOT identify the pure offering with
     the Eucharist at this verse — it does not mention the Eucharist here at all.
  ⭐ Mal 1:11 recurs as an incense warrant in TWO further notes (Ps 140/141;
     Sir 39:14), so its use is systematic in this commentary, not incidental.
  ⚠️ But note the register in CAPTURE 2: the same commentary calls incense
     "the visible sign of the prayers of all God's people" (Rev 5:8) — the
     FIGURATIVE identification the Protestant commentators make — while holding
     the physical practice. The two are not in tension for this writer.
