(a) AMERICAN BOOK OF COMMON PRAYER 1892 — DOES "INCENSE" APPEAR?
================================================================================
FINDING: YES — but only as SCRIPTURE, in an Opening Sentence of Morning Prayer.
No rubric of the 1892 Book names incense.

VERBATIM (S3, http://justus.anglican.org/resources/bcp/1892/MP_1892.htm ;
Order for Daily Morning Prayer, Opening Sentences, printed p. 1):

    "From the rising of the sun even unto the going down of the same my Name
    shall be great among the Gentiles; and in every place incense shall be
    offered unto my Name, and a pure offering: for my Name shall be great
    among the heathen, saith the LORD of hosts. Mal. i. 11."

Surrounding rubric, quoted for context (same page, immediately above the
Sentences):

    "The Minister shall always begin the Morning Prayer by reading one or more
    of the following Sentences of Scripture."

PSALTER: The justus 1892 edition's own Table of Contents entry "29. The
Psalter, or Psalms of David 329" links to the 1928 Psalter file
(http://justus.anglican.org/resources/bcp/1928/Psalms.htm), i.e. the editor
treats the 1892 and 1928 Psalters as the same Coverdale text. On that basis the
two Psalter occurrences recorded under (b) below (Ps 66:13 and Ps 141:2) are
also present in 1892. THIS IS THE EDITOR'S EQUIVALENCE CLAIM, NOT AN INDEPENDENT
VERIFICATION BY THIS PASS — flagged, see UNMATCHED §U1.

CLASSIFICATION: MENTION WITHOUT GRANT (and strictly, not even that — it is
scriptural text set for recitation, not a directory of ceremonial). Nothing in
1892 authorises, permits, or regulates the burning of incense.
CONDITIONS: none attach; the sentence is one of a set from which the Minister
selects "one or more".

2. Opening Sentences, The Order for Daily Evening Prayer, printed p. 21;

5. The Psalter, Psalm 141:2 (Domine, clamavi), extracted lines 18897-18898

    "2 Let my prayer be set forth in thy sight as the in-

(c) AMERICAN BOOK OF COMMON PRAYER 1979 — INCENSE IN ANY RUBRIC?
================================================================================
FINDING: YES. Incense is named in TWO rubrics of the 1979 Book, both in
"Additional Directions" sections, both conditional in form ("If incense is to
be used..."). Both PRESUPPOSE the use of incense and tell the officiant WHEN it
is fitting; neither grants, authorises, or permits it, and neither attaches any
condition of episcopal consent.

RUBRIC 1 — Additional Directions, "Order of Worship for the Evening",
printed p. 143. Source S4, https://bcponline.org/DailyOffice/direct.html

VERBATIM:

    "If incense is to be used, it is appropriate after the candles have been
    lighted and while the hymn Phos hilaron is being sung."

Immediate context (same page, the two paragraphs before it):

    "For the lighting of the candles at the Altar and elsewhere, in Easter
    Season the flame may be taken from the Paschal Candle. At other times, the
    candle or candles carried in at the beginning of the service may be placed
    on or near the Altar, and other candles may be lighted from them. During
    Advent, the lighting of an Advent Wreath may take place after the Prayer
    for Light. On special occasions, lighted candles may be distributed to
    members of the congregation.

    When this service is used in private houses, candles may be lighted at the
    dining table, or at some other convenient place."

RUBRIC 2 — Additional Directions, "The Dedication and Consecration of a
Church", printed p. 576. Source S4,
https://www.bcponline.org/EpiscopalServices/additional_directions_churchdedication.html

VERBATIM:

    "For the covering and decoration of the Altar, it is suitable that the
    donors of these furnishings, or other lay persons, bring them forward and
    put them in place. If incense is to be used, it is appropriate at this
    time."

Related wording on the same page (printed p. 575), showing that the 1979 Book
DOES use the word "ornaments" (contrast with 1928, item (b)):

    "When a new church is being consecrated, it is desirable that sacred
    vessels, ornaments, and decorations be carried into the building in the
    procession."

SCRIPTURAL / PSALM OCCURRENCES, DISTINGUISHED FROM RUBRICS
    - Psalm 141:2 in the 1979 Psalter ("Let my prayer be set forth in your
      sight as incense, the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice"),
      and the same verse used as a versicle/anthem at the candle-lighting.
    - Psalm 66:13 in the 1979 Psalter.
    These are text-to-be-said, not directory. They are NOT rubrics.

NEGATIVE CHECK PERFORMED: "Additional Directions" for the Holy Eucharist
(printed pp. 406-409), retrieved in full from
https://bcponline.org/HE/adddirect.html, contain NO occurrence of "incense",
"censer" or "thurible". The 1979 Book therefore does not name incense anywhere
in the eucharistic directory — only at the Evening Office and at the
consecration of a church.

COVERAGE CAVEAT: a machine-exhaustive count over the entire 1979 Book was not
achieved by this pass (see §U4). The two rubrics above are verified verbatim
against the text; the claim "these are the only two" rests on targeted section
retrieval, not on a whole-book count.

CLASSIFICATION: MENTION WITHOUT GRANT (both rubrics). The form is "If incense
is to be used" — a conditional that assumes the practice from outside the Book
and regulates only its placement in the sequence. Neither is a grant.
CONDITIONS ATTACHED: none — no bishop's direction, no permission clause, no
restriction to particular parishes. The only conditions are positional (after
the candle-lighting / during Phos hilaron; at the covering of the Altar).

================================================================================
FINDING (established directly against text, in part; see the UNMATCHED entry
§U5 for the part that is NOT):

(d.1) DIRECT TEXT SEARCH, BOS 2003 / 2018 / 2022 — front portions
Three official/near-official BOS PDFs were retrieved and searched. In every
case the retrieval was TRUNCATED by the fetch tool at roughly 105,000-108,000
characters, which corresponds to:
    BOS 2022 (S5): pages i through 91 (ends mid-"Tenebrae")
    BOS 2018 (S6): pages i through c. 97 (ends mid-Rogation-type procession)
    BOS 2003 (S7): pages i through 104 (ends at "Rogation Procession")
Within those retrieved portions, the ONLY occurrence of the string "incense"

"Anthems at the Candle Lighting (Lucernaria)", printed p. 11:

    "V. Let my prayer be set forth, O Lord:
     R. As incense in your sight."

That is Psalm 141:2 as a versicle and response — TEXT TO BE SAID, NOT A RUBRIC.
(BOS 2018 and 2022 dropped or relocated the Lucernaria section; neither
retrieved portion contained the word.)

(d.2) THE DEDICATION FORM FOR A VESSEL FOR INCENSE — EXISTENCE ESTABLISHED
The BOS section "Dedication of Church Furnishings and Ornaments" contains a
form for the dedication of a vessel for incense. Located at:
    BOS 2018, printed p. 194 (per the BOS 2018 Table of Contents, retrieved
      verbatim from the PDF text: "Dedication of Church Furnishings and
      Ornaments 194.")
    BOS 2003, in the contents as "Dedication of church furnishings and
      ornaments" (Internet Archive catalogue record for the 2003 edition,
      https://archive.org/metadata/bookofoccasional0000epis)
Attestation of the incense form, VERBATIM from TEC's own published dictionary
(S8, https://www.episcopalchurch.org/glossary/ornaments-church/):

    "The Prayer Book form for the Dedication and Consecration of a Church
    includes a general prayer of dedication for ornaments or furnishings (BCP,
    p. 573). The BOS provides specific forms for the dedication of a variety of
    church furnishings and ornaments, including forms for a cross,
    candlesticks and lamps, a service book, pictures and statues, a vessel for
    incense, and any church ornament."

*** THE EXACT WORDING OF THE BOS FORM ITSELF IS UNMATCHED BY THIS PASS. ***
See §U5. Do NOT deploy any quotation of the BOS incense form until the printed
BOS pages (2018 pp. 194ff., or the corresponding 2003/2022 pages) are obtained
and read directly.

(d.3) THE AUTHORISING INSTRUMENT FOR THE BOS ITSELF (retrieved verbatim)
From the BOS 2018 Preface, retrieved from the PDF text (S6):

    "The Book of Occasional Services is a collection of liturgical resources
    related to occasions which do not occur with sufficient frequency to
    warrant their inclusion in The Book of Common Prayer. These materials are
    authorized by the General Convention [through the provisions of Title II,
    Canon 3, Section 6 of the Canons of the Episcopal Church]. ... All of the
    material in this book is optional. None of it is required, and no
    congregation is likely to make use of all of it."

From SCLM (S11, https://www.episcopalcommonprayer.org/existing-liturgies1.html):

    "The Book of Occasional Services was authorized for optional use throughout
    this Church" by Resolution 1979-A055. It has been updated several times,
    most recently in 2022."

CONDITIONS: the BOS as a whole is authorised for OPTIONAL use throughout the
Church, and (unlike, e.g., Enriching Our Worship 6, or the expansive-language
Prayer C, which the same SCLM page marks as requiring the bishop's consent) the
BOS carries no episcopal-permission condition on its face.

CLASSIFICATION:
    - The Lucernaria versicle "As incense in your sight" (BOS 2003 p. 11):
      MENTION WITHOUT GRANT — indeed not even a mention of the ceremonial act;
      it is Psalm 141:2 recited.
    - The "vessel for incense" dedication form in "Dedication of Church
      Furnishings and Ornaments": on the evidence in hand this is
      MENTION WITHOUT GRANT — an authorised, optional form for blessing an
      object used for incense presupposes the practice and provides a rite for
      the vessel; it does not, on its face, purport to grant or license the
      burning of incense. THIS CLASSIFICATION IS PROVISIONAL until the form's
      own rubric and text are read (see §U5): if that form carries a
      conditional or permissive rubric of its own, the classification may need
      to move.

(e) TEC CANONS SINCE 1874 — HAS ANY CANON NAMED INCENSE?
================================================================================
FINDING: No TEC canon has been found that names incense. The one canon that
came closest — the Ritual Canon of 1874 — named incense ONLY in draft
specifications that were struck out; the ENACTED text does not contain the word.

(e.1) THE 1874 RITUAL CANON (§II added to Canon 20 of Title I, renumbered
Canon 22 in the Digest printed with the Journal)
Source: repo capture S12,
src/SRC_PRIMARY_1874_GeneralConvention_Journal_Canon-20-Title-I_Ritual-Canon-Sequence.txt
(built from the printed Journal of the General Convention, 1874).

That capture records, at its own §J, that the word "incense" occurs five times
in the whole OCR of the Journal: three times in successive COMMITTEE REPORTS
AND SUBSTITUTE MOTIONS (Committee report 23 Oct.; Blanchard substitute 26 Oct.;
Bolton substitute 27 Oct.), once in a House message, and once in an unrelated
Domestic Missions report ("the incense of earnest prayer"). Its finding,
verbatim from the capture:

    "The enacted text (extracts H and I) contains no occurrence of the word."

The enacted §II, as printed in the Digest with the Journal (verbatim from the
capture, OCR as found):

    "§ II. [1.] If any Bishop have reason to believe, or if complaint be made
    to him in writing by two or more of his Presbyters, that within his
    jurisdiction ceremonies or practices not ordained or authorized in the Book
    of Common Prayer, and setting forth or symbolizing erroneous or doubtful
    doctrines, have been introduced by any Minister during the celebration of
    the Holy Communion (such as. [Specifications.] a. The ele[vation]..."

and §I of the same canon (Canon 22, "Of the Use of the Book of Common Prayer"):

    "§ I. Every Minister shall, before all sermons and lectures, and on all
    other occasions of public worship, use the Book of Common Prayer, as the
    same is or may be established by the authority of the General Convention of
    this Church; and in performing such service, no other prayers shall be used
    than those prescribed by the said Book."

The rejected draft language that DID name incense (verbatim from the capture,
at the points marked "a. The use of Incense." / "1. The use of incense.") never
became canon law.

(e.2) REPEAL OF THE RITUAL CANON
Source S8, https://www.episcopalchurch.org/glossary/ritualism/ (An Episcopal
Dictionary of the Church, published by TEC), VERBATIM:

    "The General Convention of 1874 did pass a canon on ritual. ... This canon
    called for a bishop to investigate any use of ceremonies or practices in
    the bishop's jurisdiction symbolizing 'false or doubtful doctrine.' The
    bishop was empowered first to admonish and then bring to trial any member
    of the clergy who persisted in these practices. However, this canon did
    virtually nothing to slow the expansion of ritual practices in the
    Episcopal Church. Only one trial for ritualism took place. In 1877 Oliver
    Prescott received episcopal admonishment for his ritual practices. The
    canon on ritual was quietly repealed at the 1904 General Convention."

Same source on the failed earlier attempts:

    "Proposed canons on ritual were considered at the General Conventions of
    1868 and 1871, but no canons on ritual were enacted."

NOTE ON SOURCE WEIGHT: this is a secondary reference work (Armentrout & Slocum),
though published by TEC itself. The primary confirmation (the 1904 Journal /
the 1904 revision of the canons) was NOT retrieved by this pass — see §U6.

(e.3) THE INSTRUMENT THAT ACTUALLY VESTS THE DISCRETION
Source S10, White & Dykman, Annotated Constitution and Canons, extract at
https://episcopalnewsservice.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/WhiteAndDykmanOn1904Canon.pdf
VERBATIM, the canon as it stood under the numbering there ("CANON 21. Of
Ministers and Their Duties"):

    "Sec. 1 (a). The control of the worship and the spiritual jurisdiction of
    the Parish, are vested in the Rector, subject to the Rubrics of the Book of
    Common Prayer, the Canons of the Church, and the godly counsel of the
    Bishop. All other Ministers of the Parish, by whatever name they may be
    designated, are to be regarded as under the authority of the Rector.

    (b). For the purposes of his office and for the full and free discharge of
    all functions and duties pertaining thereto, the Rector shall, at all
    times, be entitled to the use and control of the Church and Parish
    buildings with the appurtenances and furniture thereof."

The same provision survives in the current canons as Title III, Canon 9,
Sec. 6(a)(1) ("The Rector or Priest-in-Charge shall have full authority and
responsibility for the conduct of the worship and the spiritual jurisdiction of
the Parish, subject to the Rubrics of the Book of Common Prayer, the
Constitution and Canons of this Church, and the pastoral direction of the
Bishop"). NOTE: this current wording was reported to this pass by web search
summary, NOT retrieved verbatim from the official C&C text — see §U7.

(e.4) FULL-TEXT SEARCH OF THE CURRENT CANONS
The 2024 Constitution and Canons, official "quotable version without
marginalia" HTML (S9,
https://extranet.generalconvention.org/staff/files/download/32150), was
retrieved and searched case-insensitively for "incense", "censer", "thurib" and
"ritual". Result: ZERO matches. HOWEVER the retrieval TRUNCATED at Title I,
Canon 5 ("Of the Archives of The Episcopal Church"), so this is a null result
over only the opening portion of Title I. It is NOT a proof of absence across
Titles I-V. See §U8.

CLASSIFICATION:
    - 1874 Ritual Canon as enacted: NOT a mention at all. It named no
      ceremonies in its operative text as retrieved; the incense
      specifications were in rejected drafts. Best classified as
      PERMISSION BY SILENCE with respect to incense — it neither named nor
      prohibited it, and even that canon was repealed in 1904.
    - Canon on the Rector's control of worship (1904 → present Title III.9.6):
      PERMISSION BY SILENCE. This is the instrument that vests discretion
      elsewhere: incense is unnamed, and the control of worship sits with the
      Rector subject to the Rubrics, the Canons, and the Bishop's counsel /
      pastoral direction.
    - No POSITIVE AUTHORIZATION of incense by name has been found in any TEC
      canon, 1874 to date.

(a) BCP 1892   "incense" present — Mal. i. 11 Opening Sentence at Morning
               Prayer (+ Psalter, on the editor's equivalence claim).
               No rubric. MENTION WITHOUT GRANT.
(b) BCP 1928   "incense" x4 (word-boundary) + "frankincense" x1, all
               Scripture: Mal. i. 11 (MP Sentences), Ps 141:2 (EP Sentences),
               Ps 66:13 and Ps 141:2 (Psalter), Matt. ii. 11 (Epiphany
               Gospel). "ornament" = 0, CONFIRMED in this digitisation.
               No rubric. MENTION WITHOUT GRANT.
(c) BCP 1979   TWO RUBRICS name incense, both conditional, both in Additional
               Directions: p. 143 (Order of Worship for the Evening) and
               p. 576 (Consecration of a Church). Plus Psalter/versicle
               occurrences, which are not rubrics.
               MENTION WITHOUT GRANT; no episcopal condition attached.
(d) BOS        Contains a form for the dedication of "a vessel for incense"
               within "Dedication of Church Furnishings and Ornaments"
               (BOS 2018 p. 194ff.; present at least from the 2003 edition).
               Existence attested by TEC's own dictionary; WORDING UNMATCHED.
               Plus Ps 141:2 as a Lucernaria versicle (BOS 2003 p. 11).
               PROVISIONALLY MENTION WITHOUT GRANT.
               BOS itself: authorised for OPTIONAL use throughout the Church
               (Res. 1979-A055; Title II.3.6), no bishop's permission required.
(e) CANONS     No TEC canon names incense. The 1874 Ritual Canon named it only
               in rejected drafts; the enacted text does not, and the canon was
               repealed in 1904. Discretion sits with the Rector under the
               Ministers'/Rectors' canon (now Title III.9.6).
               PERMISSION BY SILENCE.

OVERALL: across all five instruments, NOT ONE is a POSITIVE AUTHORIZATION of
incense by name. The 1979 Prayer Book and the Book of Occasional Services are
MENTIONS WITHOUT GRANT; the American canons are PERMISSION BY SILENCE.
