THE TWO MATERIAL DIFFERENCES
=====================================================================

DIFFERENCE 1 — WORD DIVISION AT GRIFFITHS p. 258, AND IT CHANGES THE SENSE.

  Griffiths 1859:      "the table, candlesticks, incense-ships, platters,
                        cups, mortars, and other things all of gold"
  This page:           "the table, candlesticks, incense, ships, platters,
                        cups, mortars, and other things all of gold"

  Griffiths reads a single compound noun, "incense-ships" — vessels, the
  incense-boats of the Temple furniture, rendering Jerome's "thuribula". This
  page splits it into two list items, "incense" and "ships", which produces a
  free-standing mention of incense as a substance among the Temple's golden
  furnishings, and a nonsensical "ships".

  ⭐ THIS MATTERS FOR DEPLOYMENT. On this page's reading, the sentence appears
  to name incense in its own right in a list of things "allowed of the Lord"
  under the old covenant but superseded. On Griffiths' reading it names a
  vessel, and Griffiths' own footnote goes further and says the translator got
  even that wrong — that "thuribula" means censers, not incense-ships. Anyone
  building an argument on a free-standing "incense" at this place is building
  on the weaker witness, against the scholarly edition, against that edition's
  express note, and against the Latin.

DIFFERENCE 2 — SPELLING AT GRIFFITHS p. 270, line 8.

  Griffiths 1859:      "in censing of them"
  This page:           "in sensing of them"

  Not a substantive difference; recorded because a search of this page for
  "censing" returns nothing, and a searcher who did not also try "sensing"
  would wrongly conclude the passage is absent.
