    OCR lines 262-308. Front matter, preceding the Constitution.

DECLARATION OF PRINCIPLES

OF THE

Heformeb Episcopal <JII)ui*ct).

(Adopted December 2d, 1873, and Re affirmed May 18th, 1874.)

The Reformed Episcopal Church, holding "the faith once delivered unto the
saints," declares its belief in the Holy Scriptures of the Old and New
Testaments as the Word of God, and the sole Rule of Faith and Practice ; in the
Creed " commonly called the Apostles' Creed ; " in the Divine institution of the
Sacraments of Baptism and the Lord's Supper; and in the doctrines of grace
substantially as they are set forth in the Thirty-nine Articles of Religion.

n.

This Church recognizes and adheres to Episcopacy, not as of divine right, but as
a very ancient and desirable form of church polity.

III.

This Church retaining a Liturgy which shall not be imperative or repressive of
freedom in prayer, accepts the Book of Common Prayer, as it was revised,
proposed, and recommended for use by the General Convention of the Protestant
Episcopal Church, a. d. 1785, reserving full liberty to alter, abridge, enlarge,
and amend the ■same, as may seem most conducive to the edification of the
people, "provided that tne substance of the faith be kept entire."

IV.

This Church condemns and rejects the following erroneous and strange doc¬ trines
as contrary to God's Word :

First, That the Church of Christ exists only in one order or form of
ecclesiasti¬ cal polity :

Second, That Christian ministers are "Priests" in another sense than that in
which all believers are " a royal priesthood : "

Third, That the Lord's Table is an altar on which the oblation of the Body and
Blood of Christ is offered anew to the Father :

Fourth, That the Presence of Christ in the Lord's Supper is a presence in the
elements of Bread and Wine :

Fifth, That Regeneration is inseparably connected with Baptism.

  [CAPTURE NOTE: this text is materially identical to the Declaration as read
   by Cummins on 2 December 1873 and reprinted in the Memoir (captured at
   SRC_PRIMARY_1873_Cummins_REC-Founding-Documents.txt §3), differing only in
   accidentals of spelling and pointing. The dateline here — "Adopted December
   2d, 1873, and Re affirmed May 18th, 1874" — is the Church's own attestation
   of its adoption date.]

  ⚠ NOTE THE PHRASE "erroneous and strange doctrines" in Article IV. It is the
   older Anglican formula. De Koven, arguing against the 1874 PEC ritual canon,
   objected precisely to its replacement of "the old-fashioned words, 'strange
   or erroneous doctrines' into 'erroneous or doubtful doctrines'"
   (SRC_PRIMARY_1874_DeKoven_Canon-On-Ritual-Speech_ProjectCanterbury.txt).
   The REC retained the formula De Koven preferred. Recorded as an observation;
   NOT developed, and NOT to be built into an argument without further work.

§2. CONSTITUTION, ARTICLE IX — THE ONLY CEREMONIAL PROVISION
    OCR lines 629-641. Marginal side-note: "Ritualism Pro¬ hibited."
================================================================================

ARTICLE IX.

Nothing calculated to teach — either directly or symbolically — that the
Christian Ministry pos¬ sesses a Sacerdotal character, or that the Lord's Supper
is a Sacrifice, shall ever be allowed in the worship of this Church ; nor shall
any Com¬ munion Table be constructed in the form of an altar.

  [marginal side-note printed alongside: "Ritualism Pro¬ hibited."]

  [Immediately preceding is ARTICLE VIII (Prayer Book, Articles of Religion,
   and forms; amended May 1878). Immediately following is ARTICLE X, the
   unalterability clause, also amended May 1878.]

ANALYSIS OF WHAT ARTICLE IX DOES AND DOES NOT DO — labelled as analysis, not as
the document:
  It prohibits by DOCTRINAL TEST, not by enumerated ceremony. The test is
  whether a thing teaches, "either directly or symbolically," (a) a sacerdotal
  ministry or (b) the Lord's Supper as a Sacrifice. Only ONE physical object is
  named at all: the Communion Table, which may not be built in the form of an
  altar.
  It names NO ceremonial act and NO ceremonial ornament. Not incense. Not
  candles, vestments, crucifixes, the sign of the cross, genuflection,
  elevation, or reservation.
  Its structure — a symbolic-doctrinal test with the marginal rubric "Ritualism
  Prohibited" — is formally comparable to the 1874 PEC canon's "setting forth or
  symbolizing erroneous or doubtful doctrines". The REC drafted the test and
  attached NO list of examples to it. The PEC House of Deputies drafted an
  equivalent test and DID attach a list, with incense first; the House of
  Bishops then struck the list.
