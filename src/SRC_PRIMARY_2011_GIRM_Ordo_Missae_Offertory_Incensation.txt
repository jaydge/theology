BOTTOM LINE: In the current (post-1970) Roman Rite there is NO prayer, formula,
or words of any kind prescribed for the incensation at the Preparation of the
Gifts. The rite is done in silence, and the Missal explicitly directs that even
the imposition/blessing of the incense be done "without saying anything."

GIRM §75 — 2011 ICEL English:

    "75. The bread and wine are placed on the altar by the Priest to the
    accompaniment of the prescribed formulas; the Priest may incense the gifts
    placed on the altar and then incense the cross and the altar itself, so as
    to signify the Church's offering and prayer rising like incense in the sight
    of God. Next, the Priest, because of his sacred ministry, and the people, by
    reason of their baptismal dignity, may be incensed by the Deacon or by
    another minister."

GIRM §75 — Vatican 2003 English (with US adaptations):

    "75. The bread and wine are placed on the altar by the priest to the
    accompaniment of the prescribed formulas. The priest may incense the gifts
    placed upon the altar and then incense the cross and the altar itself, so as
    to signify the Church's offering and prayer rising like incense in the sight
    of God. Next, the priest, because of his sacred ministry, and the people, by
    reason of their baptismal dignity, may be incensed by the deacon or another
    minister."

IGMR 2002 Latin, n. 75:

    "75. Panis et vinum super altare a sacerdote deponuntur comitantibus
    formulis statutis; sacerdos dona super altare collocata incensare potest,
    dein crucem et ipsum altare, ut oblatio Ecclesiæ eiusque oratio sicut
    incensum in conspectum Dei ascendere significentur. Deinde sacerdos, propter
    sacrum ministerium, et populus, ratione baptismalis dignitatis, incensari
    possunt a diacono vel ab alio ministro."

FINDING: §75 names NO prayer or formula for the incensation. The "prescribed
formulas" / "formulis statutis" it refers to are those for placing the bread and
wine ("Benedictus es, Domine…"), not for the censing.

GIRM §§276-277 — 2011 ICEL English:

    "276. Thurification or incensation is an expression of reverence and of
    prayer, as is signified in Sacred Scripture (cf. Ps 141 [140]:2; Rev 8:3).

    Incense may be used optionally in any form of Mass:

    a) during the Entrance Procession;
    b) at the beginning of Mass, to incense the cross and the altar;
    c) at the procession before the Gospel and the proclamation of the Gospel
       itself;
    d) after the bread and the chalice have been placed on the altar, to incense
       the offerings, the cross, and the altar, as well as the Priest and the
       people;
    e) at the elevation of the host and the chalice after the Consecration."

    "277. The Priest, having put incense into the thurible, blesses it with the
    Sign of the Cross, without saying anything.

    Before and after an incensation, a profound bow is made to the person or
    object that is incensed, except for the altar and the offerings for the
    Sacrifice of the Mass.

    Three swings of the thurible are used to incense: the Most Blessed
    Sacrament, a relic of the Holy Cross and images of the Lord exposed for
    public veneration, the offerings for the Sacrifice of the Mass, the altar
    cross, the Book of the Gospels, the paschal candle, the Priest, and the
    people.

    Two swings of the thurible are used to incense relics and images of the
    Saints exposed for public veneration; this should be done, however, only at
    the beginning of the celebration, following the incensation of the altar.

    The altar is incensed with single swings of the thurible in this way:

    a) if the altar is freestanding with respect to the wall, the Priest
       incenses walking around it;
    b) if the altar is not freestanding, the Priest incenses it while walking
       first to the right hand side, then to the left.

    The cross, if situated on the altar or near it, is incensed by the Priest
    before he incenses the altar; otherwise, he incenses it when he passes in
    front of it.

    The Priest incenses the offerings with three swings of the thurible or by
    making the Sign of the Cross over the offerings with the thurible before
    going on to incense the cross and the altar."

*** THE DECISIVE CLAUSE: §277's first sentence — "blesses it with the Sign of
the Cross, WITHOUT SAYING ANYTHING" — is the Missal's positive suppression of
the old "Per intercessionem beati Michaelis Archangeli…" blessing formula. ***

GIRM §144 — 2011 ICEL English:

    "144. If incense is being used, the Priest then puts some in the thurible,
    blesses it without saying anything, and incenses the offerings, the cross,
    and the altar. While standing at the side of the altar, a minister incenses
    the Priest and then the people."

GIRM §144 — IGMR 2002 Latin:

    "144. Deinde, si incensum adhibetur, sacerdos illud in thuribulo imponit,
    benedicit nihil dicens atque oblata, crucem et altare incensat. Minister
    stans ad latus altaris sacerdotem incensat, deinde populum."

GIRM §178 — 2011 ICEL English (deacon), relevant clause:

    "…If incense is being used, the Deacon assists the Priest during the
    incensation of the offerings, the cross, and the altar; and after this the
    Deacon himself or the acolyte incenses the Priest and the people."

Neither §144 nor §178 gives any formula.

ORDER OF MASS, 2011 English (Roman Missal, 3rd ed.), Preparation of the Gifts:

    "26. After this, the Priest, bowing profoundly, says quietly:

         With humble spirit and contrite heart
         may we be accepted by you, O Lord,
         and may our sacrifice in your sight this day
         be pleasing to you, Lord God.

     27. If appropriate, he also incenses the offerings, the cross, and the
     altar. A Deacon or other minister then incenses the Priest and the people.

     28. Then the Priest, standing at the side of the altar, washes his hands,
     saying quietly:

         Wash me, O Lord, from my iniquity
         and cleanse me from my sin."

n. 27 IS A PURE RUBRIC WITH NO SPOKEN TEXT. The texts the Missal DOES print at
the Preparation of the Gifts are: n. 23 "Benedictus es…" (bread); n. 24
"Per huius aquae…" ("By the mystery of this water and wine"); n. 25
"Benedictus es…" (chalice); n. 26 "In spiritu humilitatis…" ("With humble spirit
and contrite heart"); n. 28 "Lava me, Domine…" ("Wash me, O Lord"). NO INCENSE
FORMULA IS AMONG THEM.

Latin, Ordo Missae 2002 (editio typica tertia), n. 27:
    "27. Et, pro opportunitate, incensat oblata, crucem et altare. Postea vero
    diaconus vel alius minister incensat sacerdotem et populum."

Latin, Ordo Missae 1970/1975 (editio typica / altera), n. 23:
    "23. Et, pro opportunitate, incensat oblata et altare. Postea vero diaconus
    vel minister incensat sacerdotem et populum."
    [Same rubric, no formula; the 2002 edition merely added "crucem". Source for
     the 1975 text: exomni.narod.ru/ordo/vat_la.htm — a hobbyist transcription,
     corroborating only.]

VERIFIED NEGATIVE: "Incensum istud", "Dirigatur", "Accendat" and
"Per intercessionem beati Michaelis" appear NOWHERE in the 1970/1975/2002
Ordo Missae.

Evidence:
 - Full-text search of the complete Latin Ordo Missae 2002 (romanliturgy.org,
   2,557 lines, the entire Order of Mass) for "Incensum | Dirigatur | Accendat |
   Michael | Per intercessionem". The ONLY hits for "incens-" in the whole
   document are the Gospel rubrics ("Interim sacerdos incensum, si adhibetur,
   imponit"; "si incensum adhibetur, thurificat") and the offertory rubric n. 27
   above. ZERO hits for "Incensum istud", "Dirigatur", "Accendat", "Michael".
 - Same search over the full 2011 English USCCB Order of Mass PDF: none.
 - Same over the 1975 Latin Ordo Missae transcription: none.
 - Positively, GIRM §§144 and 277 replace the "Per intercessionem beati
   Michaelis" blessing with "blesses it without saying anything" / "benedicit
   nihil dicens."

These four texts belong to the 1962 Ritus servandus VII and Ordo Missae; they
have no counterpart in the reformed rite. The citable primary basis is the
promulgated books themselves (Paul VI, "Missale Romanum," 3 April 1969; Ordo
Missae, editio typica 1969/1970; Missale Romanum editio typica tertia 2002).

"SUPPLICES TE ROGAMUS" — Eucharistic Prayer I (Roman Canon), RETAINED in the OF.

Latin:
    "Supplices te rogamus, omnipotens Deus: iube haec perferri per manus sancti
    Angeli tui in sublime altare tuum, in conspectu divinae maiestatis tuae…"

2011 ICEL English, Order of Mass n. 94 (© ICEL/USCCB):

    "94. Bowing, with hands joined, he continues:

         In humble prayer we ask you, almighty God:
         command that these gifts be borne
         by the hands of your holy Angel
         to your altar on high
         in the sight of your divine majesty,
         so that all of us who through this participation at the altar receive
         the most holy Body and Blood of your Son,

     He stands upright again and signs himself with the Sign of the Cross,
     saying:

         may be filled with every grace and heavenly blessing.

     He joins his hands.

         (Through Christ our Lord. Amen.)"

*** DOES ANY CENSING ACCOMPANY IT? NO. *** GIRM §276 lists exhaustively the five
optional moments for incense, and the Supplices is not among them. The nearest
is (e) "at the elevation of the host and the chalice after the Consecration"
(GIRM §150: "If incense is used, a server incenses the host and the chalice when
each is shown to the people after the consecration"), which is EARLIER in the
Prayer, at the consecration elevations. The Order of Mass n. 94 rubric
prescribes only a profound bow and the Sign of the Cross. The same is true in
the 1962 form: the Ritus servandus provides no censing at the Supplices.

Older PUBLIC-DOMAIN English of the same clause — Nicholas Gihr, "The Holy
Sacrifice of the Mass," B. Herder, 1902 (see the separate Gihr capture file):
    "We humbly beseech Thee, Almighty God, command these to be carried by the
    hands of Thy holy Angel to Thine Altar on high, in the presence of Thy
    divine Majesty…"
