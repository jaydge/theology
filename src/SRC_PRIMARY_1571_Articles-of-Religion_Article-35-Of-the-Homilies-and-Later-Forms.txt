[W1] 1571
------------------------------------------------------------------
ARTICLE XXXV
Of Homilies

The second Book of Homilies, the several titles whereof we have joined under
this Article, doth contain a godly and wholesome Doctrine, and necessary for
these times, as doth the former Book of Homilies, which were set forth in the
time of Edward the Sixth; and therefore we judge them to be read in Churches
by the Ministers, diligently and distinctly, that they may be understanded of
the people.

⭐⭐ AND THEN, IN BRACKETS IN THE TEXT, THE AMERICAN LIMITING CLAUSE:

[This Article is received in this Church, so far as it declares the Book of Homilies to be
an explication of Christian doctrine, and instructive in piety and morals. But all references
to the constitution and laws of England are considered as inapplicable to the circumstances
of this Church; which also suspends the order for the reading of said Homilies in churches,
until a revision of them may be conveniently made, for the clearing of them, as well from
obsolete words and phrases, as from the local references.]

  SO: THE AMERICAN CHURCH RETAINED ARTICLE 35 WITH ITS TEXT INTACT AND ITS
  TITLE-LIST INTACT, AND THEN QUALIFIED IT IN TWO MOVES.
    (i) A RECEPTION LIMIT ON WHAT THE ARTICLE DECLARES: received "so far as it
        declares the Book of Homilies to be an explication of Christian
        doctrine, and instructive in piety and morals."
    (ii) A SUSPENSION OF THE DUTY: "which also suspends the order for the
        reading of said Homilies in churches, until a revision of them may be
        conveniently made".
  Note the shape of (i) with care. It limits reception TO the Homilies' being
  an explication of Christian DOCTRINE and instructive in PIETY AND MORALS. It
  does not say the Homilies are of low authority; it says the Article is
  received in respect of what the Homilies teach. And note what it does NOT
  reach: it says nothing about ritual, ceremonial, or the ornaments of the
  church, one way or the other.

2. THE PRAYER BOOK RUBRICS DIRECTING A HOMILY TO BE READ
=====================================================================

From `src/the-book-of-common-prayer-1662.pdf`, extracted with
`pdftotext -layout`, as extracted (long-s artefacts left uncorrected):

[2a] THE ORDER OF THE ADMINISTRATION OF THE LORD'S SUPPER — the standing
     rubric, immediately before the Offertory:

  ¶ Then ſhall follow the Sermon, or one of the Homilies already ſet
    forth, or hereafter to be ſet forth, by Authority.

  ⭐ THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING ONE. The Homilies stand in the 1662 Communion
  office as the appointed ALTERNATIVE TO THE SERMON, by rubric, in the body of
  the service.

[2b] The following rubric, on giving warning of the Communion, treats sermon
     and homily as interchangeable in the same breath:

  ¶ When the Miniﬅer giveth warning for the Celebration of the holy
    Communion, ... after the Sermon or Homily
    ended, he ſhall read this Exhortation following.

[2c] In the State Service for the Fifth of November:

  ¶ After the Creed, if there be no Sermon, ſhall be read one of the ſix
                        homilies againﬅ Rebellion.

[2d] In the State Service for the Thirtieth of January (King Charles the
     Martyr):

  ¶ After the Nicene Creed, ſhall be read, inﬅead of the Sermon for that
    Day, the firﬅ and ſecond parts of the Homily againﬅ Diſobedience
    and willful Rebellion, ſet forth by Authority; or the Miniﬅer, who
    officiates, ſhall preach a Sermon of his own compoſing upion the
    ſame argument.

  In [2c] and [2d] a named homily is not merely permitted but COMMANDED where
  no sermon is preached, and in [2d] a minister who preaches instead is
  required to preach on the homily's own argument.

3. THE CANONS OF 1604
=====================================================================

[W5], as transcribed at the source named above.

CANON 46. Beneficed Men, not Preachers, to procure monthly Sermons.
  "EVERY beneficed man, not allowed to be a Preacher, shall procure Sermons to
  be preached in his Cure once in every month at the least, by Preachers
  lawfully licensed, if his living, in the judgment of the Ordinary, will be
  able to bear it. And upon every Sunday, when there shall not be a Sermon
  preached in his Cure, he or his Curate shall read some one of the Homilies
  prescribed or to be prescribed by authority, to the intents aforesaid."

CANON 49. Ministers, not allowed Preachers, may not expound.
  "NO person whatsoever not examined and approved by the Bishop of the diocese,
  or not licensed, as is aforesaid, for a sufficient or convenient Preacher,
  shall take upon him to expound in his own Cure, or elsewhere, any Scripture
  or matter of Doctrine; but shall only study to read plainly and aptly
  (without glossing or adding) the Homilies already set forth, or hereafter to
  be published by lawful authority, for the confirmation of the true faith, and
  for the good instruction and edification of the people."

CANON 80. The great Bible, and Book of Common Prayer, to be had in every Church.
  "...And if any parishes be yet unfurnished of the Bible of the largest volume,
  or of the Books of Homilies allowed by authority, the said Church-wardens
  shall within convenient time provide the same at the like charge of the
  parish."

CANON 85. Churches to be kept in sufficient Reparations.
  "THE Church-wardens or Quest-men shall take care and provide that the
  Churches be well and sufficiently repaired, and so from time to time kept and
  maintained, that the windows be well glazed, and that the floors be kept
  paved, plain, and even, and all things there in such an orderly and decent
  sort, without dust or any thing that may be either noisome or unseemly, as
  best becometh the House of God, AND IS PRESCRIBED IN AN HOMILY TO THAT
  EFFECT." [emphasis added by this capture]

  ⭐⭐ CANON 85 IS THE ONE TO NOTICE. A canon of the Church of England makes the
  churchwardens' duty about the fabric and furnishing of the church conform to
  what "IS PRESCRIBED IN AN HOMILY" — i.e. to Book II Homily 3, "Of repairing
  and keeping clean of Churches". That is a homily operating PRESCRIPTIVELY, by
  canon, on a question of the ordering of the church building. Its bearing on
  the Archbishops' phrase "never been taken as having high authority on points
  of doctrine or of ritual" is argued in §4 of `Homilies_On_Incense.md`, where
  the case is put on both sides.

  ⚠ AND THE CONVERSE MUST BE RECORDED TOO. Canon 49 confines the non-preaching
  minister to reading the Homilies "without glossing or adding" — which is a
  canon treating them as MATTER TO BE READ ALOUD in place of a sermon, not as a
  code of law. Canon 46 likewise makes a homily the fallback where there is no
  sermon. Both cut toward "hortatory". Neither side of this has been resolved
  here.

4. ARTICLE XXXIV, FOR COMPARISON (the ceremonies article that stands
   immediately before Article 35 in every witness)
=====================================================================

[W1] 1571 / [W4] agree; the 1662 repo PDF agrees in substance:

  Of the Traditions of the Church

  It is not necessary that Traditions and Ceremonies be in all places one, or
  utterly alike; for at all times they have been diverse, and may be changed
  according to the diversity of countries, times, and men's manners, so that
  nothing be ordained against God's Word. Whosoever through his private
  judgement, willingly and purposely, doth openly break the traditions and
  ceremonies of the Church, which be not repugnant to the Word of God, and be
  ordained and approved by common authority, ought to be rebuked openly, (that
  other may fear to do the like), as he that offendeth against common order of
  the Church, and hurteth the authority of the Magistrate, and woundeth the
  consciences of the weak brethren.
  Every particular or national Church hath authority to ordain, change, and
  abolish ceremonies, or rites of the Church ordained only by man's authority,
  so that all things be done to edifying.

  Captured because §3 of `Homilies_On_Incense.md` turns on whether the
  Homilies' language sits inside or outside the category that Article 34 and
  "Of Ceremonies" jointly define.
