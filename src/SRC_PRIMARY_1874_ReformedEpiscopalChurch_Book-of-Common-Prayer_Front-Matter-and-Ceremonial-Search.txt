"in every place incense shall be offered unto my name, and a pure offering" —
is the SECOND OPENING SENTENCE OF MORNING PRAYER in the Reformed Episcopal
Church's own first Prayer Book. It stands second of the opening Sentences,
immediately after Habakkuk 2:20, on the first page of the first service.

VERIFIED INDEPENDENTLY, NOT INFERRED FROM A SEARCH SNIPPET. Three checks:
  (a) It is read directly off the untruncated head of the OCR, in running
      order, with the office heading and the rubric above it.
  (b) The index places the Habakkuk sentence and the Malachi sentence on the
      SAME leaf (47) — and the same pairing again on leaf 74, which is the
      corresponding page of THE ORDER FOR EVENING PRAYER. The two Sentences
      open both daily offices.
  (c) Both other searchable 1874 scans reproduce the pair: leaves 19 and 46
      in bookofcommonpray00refo_0; leaves 23 and 50 in commonpray00refo.

⚠ ATTRIBUTION DISCIPLINE. What is established is placement, and only that: the
REC printed this verse at the head of both daily offices. The book gives no
gloss on it and draws no ceremonial conclusion from it. Any argument about what
the REC thereby conceded, implied, or ought to have concluded is ANALYSIS, is
this project's own, and must be labelled as such. It is not the REC's statement
and must never be relayed as one.

⚠⚠ EDITION NOTE — MATERIAL, AND ESTABLISHED BY TEST.
The copy carries an 1874 title page and an 1874 imprint, but the Articles bound
into it were adopted at the THIRD General Council, Chicago, 18 May 1875. This
copy is therefore NOT a pure first-issue 1874 text. Three independent
confirmations that the Articles are an interpolation, not original matter:
  (i)  The Contents (EXCERPT 3) does not list them.
  (ii) They carry their own separate pagination (i-xiv+), running alongside
       the front matter's own roman sequence.
  (iii) The scanned page width changes across the section boundary — leaves
       within the Articles measure 1727 px wide, leaves of the book proper
       1686 px — consistent with a separately printed gathering.

DECISIVE TEST AGAINST THE OTHER 1874 COPY. The University of Illinois copy
(bookofcommonpray00refo_0, 612 leaves, "xvi, 588 pages") returns ZERO hits for
"Supererogation" (the title and text of Article XVI), zero for "ceremony"
(Article XXV), and zero for "cere" (Article VI). The Princeton copy returns
those hits. The Illinois copy therefore CONTAINS NO ARTICLES OF RELIGION AT
ALL. The 30-leaf difference between the two copies (642 vs 612) is the
Articles gathering.

CONCLUSION ON EDITION: the Book of Common Prayer as set forth by the Second
General Council in 1874 contained NO Articles of Religion. The Princeton copy
represents a later issue or a later binding-up, with the 1875 Articles added.

MATTER, NOT 1874 MATTER, and is marked as such below.

⭐ NOTE ON THE ARTICLE XX ANALOGUE. The REC's version reads "A CHURCH hath
power to decree Ceremonies" where the Thirty-Nine Articles read "The Church
hath power to decree Rites or Ceremonies." The indefinite article generalizes
the power to any church rather than vesting it in The Church. This is a
POSITIVE GRANT of power to decree ceremonies, subject only to the limit that
nothing be decreed contrary to God's written Word. It is not a restraint on
ceremonial and it enumerates nothing. It is also, again, 1875 matter.
