PURPOSE
  To establish the CHARACTER of the 1904 repeal of Title I, Canon 24 from the
  1904 General Convention Journal itself: whether it was a targeted removal of
  that canon on its own merits, or an incident of a general revision in which
  the canons were repealed, renumbered and re-enacted wholesale.

  This capture supplements, and does not replace,
  src/SRC_PRIMARY_1904-2018_TEC_Ritual-Canon-Repeal_and_BOS-Incense-Form.txt
  (pass 260835-60), which holds the repeal resolution itself. It also CLOSES
  that capture's UNMATCHED ITEM 2 (the House of Bishops' concurrence) —
  see EXCERPT F below.

EXCERPT A — THE INSTRUMENT WAS A JOINT COMMISSION'S WHOLESALE REVISION
================================================================================
SOURCE: Journal 1904, Appendix XIV, PRINTED PAGE 561 (read off the page foot:
        "561"), BookReaderGetTextWrapper page=604 (leaf 605).
VERBATIM (opening of the report, and its signatures):

    APPENDIX XIV.

    REPORT OF THE JOINT COMMISSION ON THE REVISION
    OF THE CANONS.

    The Joint Commission on the Revision of the Canons was continued
    at the last General Convention. It has gone carefully over its last Re-
    port both in Committee and in Commission, so far as it was not acted
    upon at the General Convention of 1901, and herewith presents the
    record of its work.

    [paragraph on the death of Dean Hoffman, the Commission's secretary, in
     1902, and on changes of membership]

    Several Canons and parts of Canons proposed in the former Report
    of the Commission were adopted at the last Convention, and these ap-
    pear in the present Report as so enacted, except Canon 11, in which
    additional provisions are now proposed. Certain slight changes have
    been made desirable by the adoption of the amended Constitution,
    and in some other respects a few of the Canons have been revised.
    As the last General Convention appointed a Joint Commission to draw
    a Canon on the subject of Provinces, and the Board of Missions re-
    ferred the whole subject of the Missionary work of the Church to a
    Committee to report to the General Convention of 1904, this Commis-
    sion makes no Report in regard to a Canon "Of Provinces," or "Of the
    Domestic and Foreign Missionary Society." The House of Deputies
    also continued its Committee on Courts of Appeal with order to report
    to the next Convention, and, therefore, this Commission has decided
    not to report a Canon on this subject.

    WM. CROSWELL DOANE, Chairman.
    CHARLES G. SAUNDERS, Secretary.

READING, STATED PLAINLY: the whole of the report's covering matter is
housekeeping — membership, what the previous Convention had already adopted,
and three subjects the Commission declined to report on (Provinces; the
Domestic and Foreign Missionary Society; Courts of Appeal). It contains NO
discussion of the ritual canon, NO reason given for dropping it, and no
reference to ceremonial of any kind.

    of the Digest be and is hereby repealed.

    Resolved, the House of Bishops concurring, That Title II., Canons 1,
    2, and 9, Section i. of the Digest be and are hereby repealed, and that in
    place thereof Canons 22 and 23, as reported by the Commission, be and
    [a] hereby enacted, with the following amendment of Canon 22, Section
    [continues on the next page]

READING: the repeal of Canon 24 is its OWN resolution, in the standard form,
but it stands as one item in an unbroken schedule of like resolutions carried
in a single message. It is neither preceded nor followed by any preamble,
recital, report or debate specific to it. Its distinguishing feature within the
schedule is the ABSENCE of the "and that in place thereof Canon N ... be and is
hereby enacted" clause that most of its neighbours carry.

The same resolution is printed a second time in the House of Deputies' parallel
record at leaf 367, in identical terms:
    "Resolved, the House of Bishops concurring, That Title I., Canon 24 of the
    Digest be and is hereby repealed."

EXCERPT D — THE NEW BODY OF CANONS, AND ITS EFFECTIVE DATE
================================================================================
SOURCE: Journal 1904, leaf 657 and leaf 815 (two printings of the last canon).
        Retrieved via full-text search, q="repealed".
VERBATIM (leaf 657):

    "These Canons shall take effect on the first day of January, in the year of
    our Lord, 1905; from and after which day all other Canons of this Church
    are hereby and shall be deemed to be, repealed; provided, that such repeal
    shall not affect any case of a violation of existing Canons committed
    before that date; but such case shall be governed by-the same law asif no
    such repeal had taken place."

VERBATIM (leaf 815, the corresponding provision in the later printing, with the
words "except as otherwise provided" added):

    "These Canons, except as otherwise provided, shall take effect on the first
    day of January, in the year of our Lord, 1905; from and after which day all
    other Canons of this Church are hereby and shall be deemed to be, repealed;
    provided, that such repeal shall not affect any case of a violation of
    existing Canons committed before that date; but such case shall be gov-
    erned by the same law as if no such repeal had taken place."

VERBATIM (leaf 814, the heading of that canon in the later printing):
    "CANON 54. Of Repealed Canons."
VERBATIM (leaf 656, the same canon in the earlier printing):
    "Canon 52. OF REPEALED CANONS."
VERBATIM (leaf 656/814, its operative sentence):
    "Whenever there shall be a repealing clause in any Canon, and the said
    Canon shall be repealed, such repeal shall not be a re-enactment of the
    Canon or Canons repealed by the said repealing clause."

READING: the Convention enacted a complete new body of canons running to
Canon 54 in its final numbering (the Commission's own report had proposed 52),
displacing the Digest entirely as of 1 January 1905.

EXCERPT G — WHAT THE 1904 CONVENTION DID ENACT TOUCHING THE CONDUCT OF WORSHIP
================================================================================
SOURCE: Journal 1904, leaf 359 (as adopted in the House of Deputies' record),
        leaf 627 ("Canon 15. OF MINISTERS AND THEIR DUTIES."), leaf 739 (the
        canon as printed with marginal notes), leaf 824 (index of canons).
        Retrieved via full-text search, q="control of the worship" and
        q="Ministers and Their Duties". PRINTED page numbers not read.
VERBATIM (leaf 739, the fullest printing, with its marginal note):

    "Of Ministers and their Duties.
     § I. [i] The control of the worship and the spirit- ual jurisdiction of
     the Parish, are vested in the Rector, subject to the Rubrics of the Book
     of Common Prayer, the Canons of the Chureh, and the godly counsel of the
     Bishop. All other Ministers of the Parish, by whatever name they may be
     designated, are to be re- garded as under the authority of the Rector."

    [marginal note:] "Control of the worship and spiritual juris- diction of
    Par- ish vested in Rector."

VERBATIM (leaf 627): "Canon 15. OF MINISTERS AND THEIR DUTIES."
VERBATIM (leaf 824, index): "§ I. [i.] Control of the worship and spiritual
    jurisdiction of Parish vested in Rector"

PROVENANCE OF THIS CANON WITHIN THE SCHEDULE: the resolution that enacted
Canons 14 and 15 reads (leaf 359, House of Deputies' record):
    "Resolved, the House of Deputies concurring, That Title I., Canons 18, 19,
    § i. [2], and Canon 20, § iii. of the Digest be and are hereby repealed,
    and that in place thereof Canons 14 and 15, as reported by the Com- [mis]
    si[on] be and are hereby enacted, in the certain amendments, as fol- OWS:"

READING, STATED PRECISELY: 1904 Canon 15 § I [i] was enacted IN PLACE OF Title
I, Canons 18, 19 § i [2] and 20 § iii of the Digest — NOT in place of Canon 24.
It is therefore not a successor to the ritual canon. It is, however, the
provision that from 1905 onward locates the control of worship, and it locates
it in the Rector, subject to the Rubrics, the Canons, and the Bishop's godly
counsel.

CAVEAT ON THE NEGATIVES. These are null results over an OCR-derived full-text
index of an ~840-leaf volume whose OCR quality is uneven (word confidences of
0-30 are common in the index and table pages). A null result here is strong
evidence of absence in the Journal's substantive text and is consistent with
the independent testimony of White & Dykman quoted below; it is not the same
thing as a page-by-page reading of the volume. It should be reported as "a
full-text search of the 1904 Journal returns no occurrence of ...", not as
"the word does not occur".

INDEPENDENT TESTIMONY ALREADY IN THE REPO, RESTATED HERE FOR CONVENIENCE
================================================================================
White & Dykman, ANNOTATED CONSTITUTION AND CANONS ... 1789-1952, vol. 1 (1954),
printed p. 411, under "CONVENTION OF 1904". Captured verbatim by pass 260835-60
at src/SRC_PRIMARY_1904-2018_TEC_Ritual-Canon-Repeal_and_BOS-Incense-Form.txt,
EXCERPT 2.3. Re-verified in this pass by full-text search of IA item
annotatedconstit01epis, q="Canon 24" (match at leaf 427):

    "Title I, Canon 24, Of the Use of the Book of Common Prayer with the ritual
    section, so called, forbidding certain ceremonies and prac- tices in the
    administration of the Holy Communion, the consideration of which, in the
    General Convention of 1871 and 1874, had threatened almost the very
    existence of the Church, was repealed by this Con- vention, and without a
    single voice being raised in its defense."

W&D is a treatise, not a primary instrument. It is used here for ONE thing that
the Journal does not itself state: the identification of Title I, Canon 24 as
the canon carrying the ritual section. That identification is now independently
established from the primary Digests — see the companion capture
SRC_PRIMARY_1874-1901_TEC_Digests_Ritual-Canon-Numbering-Trail.txt, EXCERPT 7
(Digest of 1901: "CANON 24. Of the Use of the Book of Common Prayer", with
"§ ii. — Introduction of Ceremonies or Practices not Ordained or Authorized not
Allowed"). W&D is therefore corroborative, no longer load-bearing.

V1. AN EXACT COUNT of canons repealed and of canons amended at the 1904
    Convention. Not established. See EXCERPT E and its stated method limit. The
    demonstrated floor is: the great majority of the Digest across all four
    Titles repealed and re-enacted; at least three Title I canons and one Title
    II section repealed outright; a residual clause repealing everything else
    but eight named canons; a new body of 54 canons effective 1 January 1905.

V2. PRINTED PAGE NUMBERS for EXCERPTS C, D, E, F and G. Those excerpts came
    from the full-text index, which reports scan leaf numbers only. Leaf
    numbers are given. EXCERPTS A (p. 561) and B (p. 134) were read off the

V3. WHETHER THE COMMISSION'S FULL REPORT (Appendix XIV, printed pp. 561 ff.)
    contains, later in its body, any note on the omission of the ritual canon.
    Only the report's covering matter at p. 561 was read (EXCERPT A). The body
    of the report was not read page by page. The full-text negatives for
    "ritual" and "ceremonies" cover this appendix along with the rest of the
    volume, which is why this is recorded as a gap in method rather than a live
    possibility.

V4. THE 1904 CONVENTION'S OWN DEBATE, as distinct from its Journal. General
    Convention Journals record motions, messages and votes; they do not record
    debate. The absence of debate IN THE JOURNAL is therefore weaker evidence
    than it looks, and this pass did NOT consult contemporary periodical
    reporting (e.g. The Churchman, The Living Church) for October 1904. If the
    "silently" claim is to be pressed at all, that is where it would have to be
    tested. NOT ATTEMPTED.
