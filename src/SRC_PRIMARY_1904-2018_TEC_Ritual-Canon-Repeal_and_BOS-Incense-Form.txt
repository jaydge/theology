CONDITIONS, STATED PLAINLY FROM 1.2 AND 1.3:
  - Form 18 (a vessel for incense) carries NO reservation to the bishop. Only
    forms 1 and 2 are "Reserved to the Bishop"; forms 3 and 4 are
    "Traditionally reserved to the Bishop."
  - The form is not a free-standing service: it is used "after the sermon (and
    Creed) at the Eucharist, or at the Daily Office at the time of the hymn or
    anthem following the Collects."
  - A censer is portable, so under the p. 197 rubric it "should be brought to
    the Altar and presented to the celebrant."
  - A longer form may be built by adding the presentation, versicles and
    prayers at pp. 212-213.
  - No rubric anywhere in 1.2/1.3 makes the dedication of an incense vessel
    conditional on the bishop's presence, permission, or direction.

--- EXCERPT 2.1 (the claim being tested) --------------------------------------
SOURCE: An Episcopal Dictionary of the Church, s.v. "Ritualism"
URL: E1 (https://www.episcopalchurch.org/glossary/ritualism/)
VERBATIM (the relevant sentences):

    "The General Convention of 1874 did pass a canon on ritual. ... This canon
    called for a bishop to investigate any use of ceremonies or practices in
    the bishop's jurisdiction symbolizing "false or doubtful doctrine." The
    bishop was empowered first to admonish and then bring to trial any member
    of the clergy who persisted in these practices. However, this canon did
    virtually nothing to slow the expansion of ritual practices in the
    Episcopal Church. Only one trial for ritualism took place. In 1877 Oliver
    Prescott received episcopal admonishment for his ritual practices. The
    canon on ritual was quietly repealed at the 1904 General Convention."
--- end EXCERPT 2.1 -----------------------------------------------------------

--- FINDINGS, LOOSE END 2 -----------------------------------------------------
  REPEALED (not amended, not left standing). Year: 1904, at the 41st General
  Convention, Boston.
  Instrument: House of Deputies resolution, communicated to the House of
  Bishops as Message No. 59, dated Boston, 14th day of the Session, October 21,
  1904, printed at Journal of the General Convention 1904, p. 134:
  "Resolved, the House of Bishops concurring, That Title I., Canon 24 of the
  Digest be and is hereby repealed."
  Identification of that canon as the ritual canon rests on White & Dykman,
  vol. 1, p. 411 (EXCERPT 2.3), which names "Title I, Canon 24, Of the Use of
  the Book of Common Prayer with the ritual section, so called" and says it
  "was repealed by this Convention."
  The repeal was part of the wholesale 1904 revision of the Digest of Canons,
  in which most Title I canons were repealed and re-enacted under new numbers;
  Canon 24 was repealed with nothing put in its place.

  CAVEAT ON THE NUMBERING PREMISE: the tasking described the 1874 addition as
  "§ II added to Canon 20 of Title I ... renumbered Canon 22 in that
  Convention's Digest." The first half is corroborated (W&D, EXCERPT 2.4:
  "an amendment to Title I, Canon 20, Of the Use of the Book of Common
  Prayer"). The "Canon 22" step is NOT corroborated here: the digest at D
  (stated date 1887) prints the canon as "CANON 21. — Of the Use of the Book of
  Common Prayer" with the ritual matter as its § ii, and by 1904 the same canon
  stood as Title I, Canon 24. So the chain evidenced in this pass is
  20 (1874) -> 21 (1880s digest) -> 24 (1904 digest) -> repealed 1904.
  Anyone relying on "Canon 22" should re-check it against the 1874 Digest
  itself before deploying it.

METHOD NOTE, worth keeping for future passes
  The Internet Archive exposes two endpoints that defeat the ~105k-character
  fetch ceiling, because they return one page or one search at a time:
    full-text search inside one item:
      https://<server>/fulltext/inside.php?item_id=<ID>&doc=<BASENAME>&path=<DIR>&q=<QUERY>
    one page's OCR:
      https://<server>/BookReader/BookReaderGetTextWrapper.php?path=<DIR>/<BASENAME>_djvu.xml&mode=djvu_xml&page=<N>
  <server> comes from https://archive.org/metadata/<ID>/d1 and <DIR> from
  https://archive.org/metadata/<ID>/dir (both tiny responses). <BASENAME> is the
  derivative file's base name, which is NOT always the identifier — for
  "occasional-services-2003" it is "occasional services 2003" (with spaces);
  passing the identifier instead returns
  {"error":"No hOCR or Abbyy file present"}.
  In BookReaderGetTextWrapper, page=N returns the file numbered N+1, and for
  these three books the printed page number ran one ahead of the page= value
  (page=208 -> printed 209; page=426 -> printed 411 by that book's own
  pagination; page=177 -> printed 134). Always read the page number off the
  header/footer of the returned page rather than computing it.
  Keep URLs short: the fetcher rejects long URLs with
  "cowork_web_fetch_url_too_long".
