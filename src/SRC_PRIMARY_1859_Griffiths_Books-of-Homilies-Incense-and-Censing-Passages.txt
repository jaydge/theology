WHY THIS EDITION: Griffiths 1859 is the standard scholarly edition of the two
  Books of Homilies — it prints the text with collated variants from the
  sixteenth-century editions in the footnotes, supplies the sources of the
  patristic and classical quotations in Latin/Greek footnotes, and carries a
  full index and a glossary of archaic words. The glossary and index are used
  below as INTERNAL FINDING AIDS: they independently confirm page-and-line
  locations for `censing` and for `incense-ships`, which is how the OCR-derived
  locations below were checked.

CAPTURE METHOD: the full 2,323,646-character text layer was loaded into a
  browser tab and searched in-page with JavaScript regular expressions; only
  matched passages were extracted. Two search passes were run:
    (1) literal terms — incense, censing, censer, cense, perfume, odour,
        frankincense, sweet savour, savour, thurib-, sensing;
    (2) a de-hyphenated pass, in which the whole text was normalised by
        `.replace(/-\s*\n\s*/g,'').replace(/\s+/g,' ')` before searching, plus
        OCR-tolerant patterns for the common confusions (n/u, c/e, i/l, s/f,
        rn/m, sh/sb).
  PASS 2 WAS NECESSARY AND IT CHANGED THE RESULT. Pass 1 alone missed two real
  occurrences: `iucense-sbipa` (OCR of "incense-ships", p. 258) and "one crumb
  of in-\ncense" (hyphenated across a line break, p. 266). Any count derived
  from a naive grep of this OCR file will therefore be WRONG AND LOW. The
  figures in `Homilies_On_Incense.md` §1 are the pass-2 figures.

  - All other "savour" hits (11) are figurative — "savour of death unto death",
    "sweet savouriness", "savouring and tasting not of the flesh but of the
    Spirit", "they neither savour God nor any godliness". None is the sacrificial
    "sweet savour" of Leviticus/Ephesians. Recorded here so the absence is on
    the record rather than merely unmentioned.
