================================================================================
§0. WHY THIS IS SECONDARY, AND WHERE IT MUST NOT BE USED
================================================================================

Aycrigg's book is a dated chronicle digesting newspaper reports. Its Preface
(printed pp. 3-4) sets out an abbreviation key for the papers it draws on:
(Epis.) Episcopalian of Philadelphia; (Ch. St.) Church and State of New York;
(Prot. Ch.) Protestant Churchman; (Chn.) Hartford Churchman; (Ch. Jo.) Church
Journal of New York; (So. Ch.) Southern Churchman; (St. X.) Standard of the
Cross; (Obs.) New York Observer; (Trib.) New York Tribune; (Times); (Post);
(Herald); (Rock).

⚠ CRITICAL INTERPRETIVE POINT, ESTABLISHED IN THIS PASS. The lower-case roman
numerals trailing Aycrigg's entries ("xii. 56, 58", "iii. Oct. 23", "xi. 43")
are INTERNAL CROSS-REFERENCES TO CHAPTERS OF AYCRIGG'S OWN BOOK. They are NOT
periodical volume citations. The actual newspaper source, where he gives one, is
only ever the parenthetical abbreviation attached to the catchword.

CONSEQUENCE: the Chapter III entries covering the 1874 House of Deputies ritual
debate (printed pp. 70-75) carry NO parenthetical newspaper abbreviation at all
— only internal cross-references. AYCRIGG DOES NOT NAME HIS SOURCE FOR THE
DEBATE QUOTATIONS. He does name sources for adjacent material (the Republic of
Oct. 21; Trib. of Nov. 2; Ch. St.; Epis.; Churchman) and cites "the official
report" at p. 156.

⚠ SUPERSESSION. The two 1874 floor speeches Aycrigg reports (Andrews of Ohio,
Hall of Long Island) have since been located in the CONTEMPORARY STENOGRAPHIC
RECORD — "Debates of the House of Deputies ... 1874", IA item
"debatesofhouseof00epis_0", captured in this staging folder as
SRC_PRIMARY_1874_Debates-House-of-Deputies_Ritual-Canon-Speeches.txt.
THAT VOLUME GOVERNS. Aycrigg's versions are demonstrably lossy — his Andrews
text drops both the geographical qualifier and half the conditional's protasis.
DO NOT QUOTE AYCRIGG FOR EITHER SPEECH.

WHAT THIS FILE IS STILL GOOD FOR: (a) Aycrigg's own contemporary-adjacent REC
reading of the 1874 canon's outcome, which is itself evidence of how
Evangelicals understood the result; (b) the Bishop H. W. Lee address, for which
he names a source and for which no better text has been located; (c) his
explicit statement that the Bishops' reason is unknowable.

§1. ⚠ AYCRIGG'S ACCOUNT OF THE STRIKING IS INACCURATE — CORRECTION RECORDED
================================================================================

Aycrigg states repeatedly (printed pp. 70, 74, 78, 156) that the House of
Bishops struck TWO examples — incense and the crucifix — leaving the other two.

THAT IS NOT WHAT THE JOURNAL RECORDS. Per the repo's own capture
(src/SRC_PRIMARY_1874_GeneralConvention_Journal_Canon-20-Title-I_Ritual-Canon-Sequence.txt,
extract F, printed p. 333), the Bishop of North Carolina moved:

  "Resolved That § II. of the Canon on Ritual be amended by striking out the
  words beginning with 'And as examples,' and ending with 'Book of Common
  Prayer.'"  Which was adopted.

That is the WHOLE parenthesis — ALL FOUR examples. The elevation and adoration
clauses reappear in the enacted canon only because the COMMITTEE OF CONFERENCE
reinserted them, in altered wording and under "such as" rather than "as
examples ... declared to be considered as such" (extract H, printed p. 183).

Aycrigg is describing the NET EFFECT across the whole legislative sequence, not
the Bishops' actual motion. His shorthand is a trap: it makes the Bishops look
as though they singled out incense and the crucifix for deletion on their own
merits, when what they in fact did was delete the entire exemplary apparatus.
RECORDED SO THE ERROR IS NOT INHERITED.

================================================================================
§2. AYCRIGG ON WHY THE BISHOPS STRUCK — HE SAYS HE CANNOT KNOW
    Chapter XII, 59th Section. Printed p. 156.
================================================================================

VERBATIM:

"Canon on Ritual (iii. Oct. 23 to 31, 1874). This proves that the Ritualists
controlled the General Convention in some way that can only be surmised, since
the House of Bishops acts in secret. In (iii. Nov. 14, 1874, Ritualist) some of
the thirty-two points enumerated are beyond my knowledge. But the official report
shows these simple facts. The House of Deputies wished to prohibit " Incense "
and " Crucifix " in express terms. The House of Bishops refused. The Deputies
insisted, and appointed a Committee of Conference. The Deputies yielded to the
Bishops, and the words " Incense " and " Crucifix " were stricken from the Canon,
and in this form the Canon was passed by ayes, 28 Clerical, and 28 Lay votes, to
noes, 2 Clerical, and 1 Lay vote; or total, 56 to 3. So, that by an almost
unanimous vote of the General Convention, the Ritualists are now officially
informed that they may use Incense and the figure of Christ suspended on the
Cross, without the danger of being charged with wrong."

  [⚠ Aycrigg's "28 Clerical" here is inconsistent with his own p. 74 figure of
   38 clerical and with the Journal, which records 38 clerical ayes. Treat his
   vote figures as unreliable throughout; use the Journal.]

VALUE OF THIS PASSAGE: an REC-approved 1880 source, hostile to the outcome,
states in terms that the Bishops' reason "can only be surmised, since the House
of Bishops acts in secret," and that his evidentiary basis is "the official
report" — i.e. the Journal, which gives sequence and votes but no reasons. This
is independent corroboration that NO CONTEMPORARY REASON WAS ON THE RECORD.

================================================================================
§3. THE RITUALIST'S ACCOUNT — MECHANICS WITHOUT A REASON
    Chapter III, entry of 14 November 1874. Printed pp. 77-78.
    Aycrigg's cited source: (Trib.) = New York Tribune, a signed letter over
    the pseudonym "Ritualist".
================================================================================

The letter enumerates thirty-two "counts" against Ritualists, of which
"29. Crucifixes.— 30. Incense.— 31. Elevation of sacred species.— 32.
Eucharistic Adoration." VERBATIM continuation:

"All these had been specified. But to the amazement of Catholics 28 out of the
32 counts had been utterly abandoned, and the fight was to close around the last
four only. Even the use of the crucifix and of incense at other times than the
celebration was not touched. . . .So the Canon passed and went to the House of
Bishops. . . .The latter struck their pen through. . . .the two. . . .concerning
crucifixes and incense. . . .In this form the Canon passed both houses. . .
.leaving two only. . . .The first of the two is— not any elevation whatever,
but— ' the elevation of the elements in the Holy Communion in such a manner as to
expose them to the view of the people as objects towards which adoration is to be
made' It is something which is not and has not been the practice in any
ritualistic Church. So that this specification was uselessly inserted. This
reduces the entire indictment to one count only— namely, no adoration of the
blessed Lord except such as are provided by the Rubrics of the Prayer Book. But
as Dr. De Koven said : ' That is all we ask ;' the Prayer Book says : ' All
kneeling.' And then the Canon went through with an almost unanimous vote."

  [Aycrigg's bracketed note: this letter, "and one by a Reformed Episcopalian
   (H. B. Turner), are printed together under the title 'Church Freedom' (pp.
   16), ace. R. E. C. at 38 Bible House, New York." That pamphlet was searched
   for on the Internet Archive in this pass and NOT FOUND — see the main
   findings file, §2 search record.]

FINDING: this account, from the Ritualist side, confirms the mechanics of the
striking and gives NO reason for it either. Note also its own observation that
the canon as drafted reached only "the celebration", leaving "the use of the
crucifix and of incense at other times than the celebration ... not touched" —
which independently corroborates De Koven's structural point of 31 October.

§4. BISHOP H. W. LEE — INCENSE COUPLED WITH SYMBOLIC DOCTRINE
    Chapter II, entry of 13 June 1874. Printed p. 48.
    Aycrigg's cited source: (Chn.) = Hartford Churchman, reporting Bishop Lee's
    address to the Iowa diocesan Convention, 26 May 1874.
    ⚠ SECONDARY AT TWO REMOVES: a newspaper report of a diocesan address,
    reprinted in an 1880 chronicle. Not a General Convention document.
================================================================================

VERBATIM (the relevant sentence):

"Who can doubt that such things as Eucharistic adoration, auricular confession,
invocation of the Virgin Mary, prayers for the dead, vestments which symbolize
peculiar Eucharistic doctrines, a scenic and sensuous worship with crossings and
genuflections, lights and incense, and other kindred matters, are in entire
opposition to the true spirit and history of the Church ; and that they, if
permitted and practiced, would eat out its very vitals as a Reformed and
Primitive Church, and sap its foundations as an Apostolic body, and as one
restored from the errors and corruptions of ages of darkness and superstition ?"

and, closing the same address:

"An important crisis is upon us as a Church, or else is rapidly approaching, and
let us meet it in the fear of God and with a firm determination."

CAREFUL READING, labelled as analysis: Lee's metaphor claims that ritualism would
destroy the Church AS Reformed, Primitive and Apostolic — a change of character,
not an end of existence. He is also the nearest thing found to a sitting American
bishop, in 1874, naming incense in a list of things opposed to the Church's true
spirit. But note he names it in a list, with lights, and it is the VESTMENTS he
qualifies as symbolizing doctrine, not the incense.
