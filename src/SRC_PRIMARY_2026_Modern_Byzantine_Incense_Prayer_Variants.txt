This file collates MODERN (mostly IN-COPYRIGHT) published English renderings of
the one short Byzantine Prayer of the Incense, for comparison of wording only.
Each entry gives its own source URL and copyright status. Filename year 2026 =
year of capture, since the entries span 1984-2025.
=============================================================================

[1] Nicon D. Patrinacos, "A Dictionary of Greek Orthodoxy — Λεξικον Ελληνικης
    Ορθοδοξιας." Minnesota: Light & Life Publishing, 1984, p. 205, s.v.
    "Incense (Greek: θυμίαμα)". Quoted at https://orthodoxwiki.org/Incense
    (retrieved 2026-09-05). UNDER COPYRIGHT.

    "We offer to Thee, Christ our God, this incense as a spiritual fragrance;
    receive it, we pray, to Thy heavenly altar and send down to us, in return,
    the grace of Thy Holy Spirit."

    Altar clause: "to Thy heavenly altar". Verb: receive.

CROSS-EDITION SUMMARY OF THE ALTAR CLAUSE AND VERB
  Covel 1722, p. 17          "thy Altar above the Heavens"            received (ptcp)   PD
  Neale 1859, p. 169         "Thy heavenly altar"                      receive (imper.)  PD
  Robertson 1894, p. 217     "thy heavenly Altar"                      accepting (ptcp)  PD
  Hapgood 1906 p.75/1922 p.74 "thy most heavenly Altar"                accept (imper.)   PD
  Patrinacos 1984, p. 205    "Thy heavenly altar"                      receive           ©
  St Tikhon's 1984/2010      "Thy heavenly altar"                      Receive (imper.)  ©
  Lash / Thyateira           "your altar above the heavens"            Accept (imper.)   ©
  AGES / GOA / Dedes         "Your altar which is above the heavens"   accept            ©
  ROCOR EAD sheet            "Thy most heavenly altar"                 accepting (ptcp)  ©
  ponomar "Royster"          "thy most heavenly altar"                 accept (imper.)   ©
  Melkite / Raya             "Your altar in heaven"                    receive           ©

EVERY located rendering, without exception, contains (a) the verb "offer" with
incense as its direct object, and (b) a petition that God accept/receive that
incense at a heavenly altar.

PATTERN WORTH LOGGING: the words "noetic," "spiritual," "super-celestial" and
"supersensual" never appear in THIS prayer in any rendering located. They belong
to the neighbouring post-consecration litany petition (Hapgood: "holy, and most
heavenly, and supersensual Altar"; Neale: "holy and super-celestial and
intellectual altar"; Covel p. 133: "Holy, and Supercelestial, and Intellectual
Altar"; Jordanville/HTM: "holy and most heavenly and noetic altar"). Anyone
citing a "noetic altar" for the censer prayer is conflating the two texts.
