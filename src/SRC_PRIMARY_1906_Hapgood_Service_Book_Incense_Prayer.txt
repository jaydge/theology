(1) PRAYER OF THE CENSER — Liturgy of St John Chrysostom, Office of Oblation
    (Proskomide / Prothesis), immediately after the particles are gathered on
    the paten and before the covering of the Gifts.

1906 ed., p. 75. Rubric and prayer as printed:

    "Then the Deacon taketh the censer, and having placed incense therein, he
    saith to the Priest :
        Bless, Master, the censer.
    And straightway he saith :
        Let us pray to the Lord.

    Then the Priest saith the PRAYER OF THE CENSER.

        Unto thee, O Christ our God, do we offer incense for an odour of
        spiritual fragrance : which do thou accept upon thy most heavenly
        Altar, and pour forth upon us in return the grace of thine all-holy
        Spirit."

    [Note: the mark after "fragrance" is a SPACED COLON ( : ) in the 1906
    printing. "Altar" is capitalised; "heavenly" is not.]

1922 revised ed., p. 74. Same prayer, ONE substantive change:

        "Unto thee, O Christ-God, do we offer incense for an odour of
        spiritual fragrance: which do thou accept upon thy most heavenly
        Altar, and pour forth upon us in return the grace of thine all-holy
        Spirit."

    Differences 1906 -> 1922:
      a. "O Christ our God" -> "O Christ-God"   (the only wording change)
      b. spaced colon -> close colon (French spacing dropped)
      c. p. 75 -> p. 74; the 1922 sets the Liturgy in parallel columns
      d. Rubric heading unchanged: "Then the Priest saith the PRAYER OF THE CENSER."

Immediately following on the same page (1906, p. 75), the censer is used at once:

    "Then the Priest, having censed the star-cover, placeth it over the Holy
    Bread, saying : And the Star came and stood over the place where the young
    Child was."
    "Then, taking the censer, the Priest censeth the Table of Oblation, saying
    thrice : Blessed art thou, O our God, who herein art well pleased. Glory to
    thee."

(2) PRAYER OF THE CENSER — pontifical form, before the Gospel. 1906 ed., p. 88,
    right-hand (pontifical) column:

    "If a Bishop be the celebrant, the Proto-Deacon taketh the censer, and a
    Deacon the incense, and they approach the Bishop. And the Bishop, putting
    incense into the censer, reciteth the while the Prayer of the Censer.

        We offer unto thee the censer, O Christ our God, for the savour of a
        sweet spiritual odour; which do thou accept upon thy most heavenly
        Altar, requiting us with the grace of thy Holy Spirit."

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
(3) PRAYER OF OBLATION — 1906 ed., p. 76 (reprinted p. 80 in the pontifical
    column). Said with the censer in the deacon's hand:

    "Then both make three devout reverences. And the Deacon, taking the censer,
    saith : For the Precious Gifts now offered up, let us pray to the Lord.
    Then the Priest maketh the Prayer of Oblation :

        O God our God, who didst send forth the Heavenly Bread, the Nourishment
        of the whole world, our Lord and God, Jesus Christ, to be our Saviour
        and Redeemer and Benefactor, blessing and sanctifying us : Do thou, the
        same Lord, bless also this oblation, and accept it on thy most heavenly
        Altar. Call to remembrance those who offer it, and those for whom it is
        offered, inasmuch as thou art good and lovest mankind ; and preserve us
        blameless in the holy ministry of thy Divine Mysteries."

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
(4) POST-CONSECRATION LITANY — 1906 ed., p. 111 (page number reconstructed from
    the running-head sequence; treat as high-confidence but not directly
    legible). NO CENSING accompanies this petition:

    "For the Precious Gifts which have been offered and sanctified, let us pray
    to the Lord.
    That our God, who loveth mankind, will accept them upon his holy, and most
    heavenly, and supersensual Altar, for an odour of spiritual fragrance ; and
    will send down upon us in return his divine grace and the gift of his Holy
    Spirit, let us pray to the Lord."

    Parallel in the Liturgy of the Presanctified, 1906 ed., p. 140:

    "That our God, who loveth mankind, accepting them upon his holy and most
    heavenly and supersensual Altar, in the odour of a spiritual sweet savour,
    will send down upon us in return his divine grace and the gift of the Holy
    Spirit"

    [Hapgood's word for the noetic / hyper-heavenly altar is "supersensual"
    (also printed "super-sensual"). The word "noetic" does NOT occur anywhere in
    the volume.]

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
(5) PRAYER OF THE OBLATION after the Great Entrance, ST BASIL alternative,
    1906 ed., p. 98 (reconstructed page number):

        "...which do thou accept upon thy holy, and heavenly, and supersensual
        Altar for the savour of a sweet odour."

    The Chrysostom form in the parallel column reads only:
        "Accept also the prayer of us sinners, and bear it to thy holy Altar".

NOTE ON RUBRICS AT VESPERS AND MATINS: Hapgood prints NO prayer over the incense
at Vespers or Matins — only the censing rubrics and the psalm verse "Let my
prayer be set forth in thy sight as incense, and let the lifting up of my hands
be an evening sacrifice." The "most heavenly Altar" language appears ONLY in the
Liturgy (and, outside the Liturgy, in the Consecration of a Church, p. 497, and
in two Thanksgiving offices).
