PURPOSE
  To establish, from Digests of the Canons of record rather than from secondary
  summaries, the canon-number under which the American ritual canon of 1874
  ("Of the Use of the Book of Common Prayer", § ii) stood at each point between
  its enactment in 1874 and its repeal in 1904.

  Pass 260835-60 recorded the trail as UNVERIFIED and offered
  20 -> 21 (1880s digest) -> 24 (1904), while the commissioning brief for that
  pass had asserted 20 -> 22 -> 21 -> 24. Neither is what the dated Digests
  show. See FINDING below.

METHOD NOTE (carried forward from 260835-60, confirmed again here)
  Two Internet Archive endpoints defeat the ~105k-character fetch ceiling by
  returning one search or one page at a time:
    full-text search inside one item:
      https://<server>/fulltext/inside.php?item_id=<ID>&doc=<BASENAME>&path=<DIR>&q=<QUERY>
    one page's OCR:
      https://<server>/BookReader/BookReaderGetTextWrapper.php?path=<DIR>/<BASENAME>_djvu.xml&mode=djvu_xml&page=<N>
  <server> from https://archive.org/metadata/<ID>/d1 ; <DIR> from
  https://archive.org/metadata/<ID>/dir .
  RELATION OF THE TWO PAGE INDICES: the "page" value returned by the
  fulltext/inside.php search is the scan LEAF index; BookReaderGetTextWrapper
  page=N returns leaf N+1. The PRINTED page number is neither of these and was
  read off the running head/foot of the returned page wherever it is stated
  below. Where it is not stated, it was not read, and is not asserted.

  All excerpts below are Internet Archive OCR, page by page or via the item's
  own full-text index. Treat as HIGH-CONFIDENCE-OCR, not a proofread collation.

    Of the Use of the Booh of Common Prayer.
    § I. Every Minister shall, before all sermons and lectures, and on all

Convention and is stated on the face of that Convention's own Digest.

FINDING — THE TRAIL, AS THE DATED DIGESTS ACTUALLY SHOW IT
================================================================================
  1874  enacted as an added § II to Title I, CANON 20; printed in the Digest
        issued with that same Convention's Journal as CANON 22.       [D1874]
  1880  CANON 22                                                      [D1880]
  1884  CANON 22                                                      [D1884]
  1887  CANON 21   (Sundays canon is 20 on the same page)              [D1887]
  1889  CANON 22   (Sundays canon is 21 on the same page)              [D1889]
  1896  CANON 24                                                      [D1896]
  1901  CANON 24, § ii still the ritual section                       [D1901]
  1904  Title I, CANON 24 repealed                                    [1904 Jnl]

  The canon's TITLE ("Of the Use of the Book of Common Prayer"), its § i
  (requiring use of the Book), and its § ii (the ritual section) are continuous
  and unchanged in substance across every one of these dated points.

  THE NUMBER MOVES BECAUSE CANONS AHEAD OF IT WERE REPEALED OR ADDED, NOT
  BECAUSE THIS CANON WAS EVER RE-ENACTED. That is demonstrated on the face of
  D1887 and D1889: in both, this canon sits immediately after "Of the Due
  Celebration of Sundays", and both canons shift together (20/21 in 1887,
  21/22 in 1889).

  CORRECTION TO THE TRAIL AS PREVIOUSLY STATED. Neither "20 -> 22 -> 21 -> 24"
  nor "20 -> 21 -> 24" is right as a chronological sequence. The 21 reading is
  real but belongs to ONE dated Digest (1886/87) and is bracketed on BOTH sides
  by 22. Anyone deploying a numbering claim should say 20 (1874, at once 22),
  then 22, briefly 21 in 1886/87, 22 again in 1889, 24 from 1895/96, repealed
  1904 — or, better, should not deploy the numbering at all and should say
  instead that the canon kept one title and one text throughout and changed
  number only as its neighbours changed.

UNMATCHED
================================================================================
U1. THE INSTRUMENT OF EACH RENUMBERING. Not established for any step after
    1874. What is established is the STATE at each dated Digest, not the
    resolution (if any) that produced it. No Convention resolution renumbering
    this canon was located for 1886, 1889, or 1892/1895.
    NOTE, as evidence that such resolutions did exist as a class: the 1904
    Journal, p. 134, contains a free-standing renumbering resolution in this
    form — "Resolved, the House of Bishops concurring, That Title I., Canon 13
    of the Digest be numbered Canon 20." So renumbering by express resolution
    was an available instrument in this period. Whether the 1886, 1889 and
    1892/1895 shifts were effected that way, or by the compilers of the Digest,
    was NOT determined.
    Searched: the 1904 Journal (full-text) for renumbering language; the dated
    Digests themselves, which print the result and not the instrument. The
    Journals of 1886, 1889, 1892 and 1895 were NOT searched for renumbering
    resolutions; the 1889 volume was searched only for the canon heading.

U2. DIGESTS FOR 1877, 1883, 1892, 1898. Not consulted. No IA item was located
    for a General Convention Journal of 1877, 1892, 1895 or 1898 under the
    "journalofgeneral<year>unse" naming that succeeded for 1901 and 1904;
    metadata probes for journalofgeneral1877unse, journalofgeneral1892unse and
    journalofgeneral1898unse all returned "Couldn't locate item". The gaps at
    1877 and 1883 are bracketed by 1874/1880 and 1880/1884 respectively, all
    reading 22, so no unobserved excursion is likely there; the gap 1889->1896
    is the one in which the move to 24 occurred and it is NOT resolved to a
    single Convention.

U3. PRINTED PAGE NUMBERS for D1880, D1884, D1896 and D1901 excerpts. Those
    excerpts were taken from each item's full-text index, which reports scan
    leaf numbers only. The leaf numbers are given above; the printed page
    numbers were not read off the pages and are not asserted. (D1887 p. 89 and
    D1889 p. 85 WERE read off the pages themselves.)
