§1. MR. ANDREWS, of Ohio (lay deputy) — 27 October 1874
    IA leaf 237. Printed page reported elsewhere as 225 — UNVERIFIED.
    Retrieved by query q="break up this Church" (1 match in the volume).
================================================================================

CONTEXT: this is the passage the brief's ITEM 1(e) asks for — a Deputy other
than De Koven connecting incense to the doctrinal/symbolic question. It falls on
the day the House of Deputies adopted the Committee's resolution containing the
four examples.

VERBATIM:

"Now, what is the feeling of this Church through- out all the length and breadth
of this land in refer- ence to these matters. Suppose that this Conven- tion,
expressly or impliedly, were to give voice to the opinion that incense' in the
a(Sninistration of the Holy Communion is admissible, and on Christmas next,
throughout all the length and breadth of this land, when we come forward to the
celebration of the Lord's Sup- per and present our bodies a living sacrifice
holy and acceptable unto God, sm-pliced boys enter these chancels and wave the
censer, what do you suppose the effect in this Chm'ch would be ? I ask you, gen-
tlemen. I do not know what it would be here in New York ; I cannot tell : but
throughout thi^ whole continent west of this city, if that one thing was
authorized and it was un- derstood that this Church had committed itseK
deliberately to it, I know well that it would break up this Church, and this
Convention would never sit again. You would not have to discuss the question
whether there could be a Church without a Bishop ; but you would have to discuss
the ques- tion whether there should be Bishops without a Church. There is no
mistake about this. It may seem strange to many living under different
influences ; but there is no mistake about it. I appeal to my breth- ren without
any distinction of Church, High or Low, and I say here that I have just as much
faith in my High Church brethren on a question affecting the life of this Church
as I have in my Low Church brethren."

--- NOTE: AYCRIGG'S VERSION IS LOSSY. DO NOT QUOTE IT. ---
Aycrigg, "Memoirs of the Reformed Episcopal Church" (1880), printed p. 71,
renders this as: "objects to indirect teaching by symbols and not words.
'Suppose that this Convention expressly or impliedly were to give voice to the
opinion that incense....is admissible, and on Christmas next throughout....this
land....surpliced boys enter these chancel»s and wave the censer.... It would
break up this Church, and this Convention would not sit egain.'"

Three material differences from the stenographic text:
  (a) "would not sit egain" for "would never sit again";
  (b) Aycrigg DROPS Andrews's geographical qualifier entirely — "I do not know
      what it would be here in New York ; I cannot tell : but throughout thi^
      whole continent west of this city";
  (c) Aycrigg DROPS the conditional's full protasis — "if that one thing was
      authorized and it was un- derstood that this Church had committed itseK
      deliberately to it".
Aycrigg's compression converts a doubly-hedged conditional into something much
closer to a flat prediction. Quote the Debates text, not Aycrigg's.

--- WHAT THIS PASSAGE IS AND IS NOT ---
It is a CONDITIONAL. Andrews does not say that incense as then practised was
breaking up the Church. He says the Church would break up IF the Convention
"expressly or impliedly" declared incense admissible AND that authorization were
acted on nationwide. No such authorization was proposed by anyone, and the
Convention moved in the opposite direction. Anyone deploying this passage must
carry the conditional with it.

§2. REV. DR. HALL, of Long Island — 27 October 1874
    IA leaf 256. Printed page reported elsewhere as 244 — UNVERIFIED.
    Retrieved by query q="downfall or the victory" (1 match in the volume).
================================================================================

VERBATIM:

"Now, gentlemen, in all cases of this kind the ques- tion is whether you pass a
Canon that means some- thing or not. That is the point. The Canon may be
imperfect. The Canon must meet all the points. The Canon may be capable of the
perversion of my eloquent friends, and yet the Canon may exactly meet the point
; and outside of this Church it is re- cognized as meeting the point. Outside of
this building it is perfectly well understood w'hat the Canon means. It means
either the downfall or the victory of Ritualism."

§3. FURTHER MATERIAL IN THIS VOLUME — LOCATED BUT NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED
================================================================================

The following were reported to this pass from the same volume but were NOT
re-verified by independent query. They are recorded as LEADS, with their
reported locations, so a later pass can confirm or discard them. DO NOT QUOTE
ANY OF THESE OUTWARD UNTIL VERIFIED.

  LEAD 1 — Rev. Dr. FULTON, of Alabama, opening for the Committee on Canons,
    Monday 26 October 1874, reported printed pp. 194-195. Reported wording
    includes: "a great excitement of public opinion"; "while other bodies might
    divide, might split into fragments ... our Church has been before the public
    constantly ... whatever centrifugal influences it may include, the
    centripetal is stronger than all"; "There has been a deep-seated feeling of
    uneasiness in the Church itself"; "the evidence of anxiety was very manifest
    in the deluge of petitions".
    SIGNIFICANCE IF CONFIRMED: the deputy managing the canon framed the crisis
    as excitement/uneasiness/anxiety and expressly denied the centrifugal
    reading. Bears directly on ITEM 2.

  LEAD 2 — Mr. RUGGLES, of New York, 30 October 1874, reported printed
    pp. 324-325, on the PROVINCIAL system (not ritual): "the provincial system,
    which involves the ruin of the Church"; "the proposition can lead to nothing
    but disruption, dismemberment and total ruin"; "the ruin of this Church
    would be a greater calamity to mankind than the r[u]in of the Union itself."
    SIGNIFICANCE IF CONFIRMED: shows the existential register was available and
    in active use in this House in this session, on a different subject.

  LEAD 3 — Mr. McCRADY, of South Carolina, reported printed p. 322, on reducing
    the size of the House: "you begin to destroy this Convention, and you begin
    to destroy this Church."

  LEAD 4 — Rev. Dr. SULLIVAN, of Illinois, 29 October 1874, reported printed
    p. 294: "Because they ask only a small thing, we are told that the granting
    of this small thing would be the entering in of the wedge; that, in other
    words, in the end it would split and break up the Church."

  LEAD 5 — Rev. Dr. HUNTINGTON, of Massachusetts, same page, reported: "because
    we are in a crisis ... What is the crisis? ... To my mind, the crisis comes
    of a far prof[o]under movement than that. It comes of a movement bo[r]n by
    Divine Providence forty years ago in the marvellous brain of that
    illustrious man, John Henry Newman."

  LEAD 6 — an unnamed deputy, long-serving Chairman of the Committee on Canons,
    reported printed p. 102: "the public, who suppose we are goin[g] to be split
    into ten thousand pieces by these subjects; but I do not believe we shall
    lose a hair b[y] them."
