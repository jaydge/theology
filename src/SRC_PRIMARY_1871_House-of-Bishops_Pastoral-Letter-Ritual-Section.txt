WHY THIS DOCUMENT IS THE HIGH-VALUE TARGET FOR ITEM 2.
  In the House of Bishops on 29 October 1874, the Bishop of Maryland moved a
  substitute resolution declining to concur in the ritual canon, whose reasoning
  included that "this House, referring to its Pastoral Letter issued in the last
  General Convention as its own action, is well satisfied to rest upon the
  response made to such teaching by the vote of the House of Deputies."
  (Journal 1874, printed p. 334; captured in the repo at
  src/SRC_PRIMARY_1874_GeneralConvention_Journal_Canon-20-Title-I_Ritual-Canon-Sequence.txt,
  extract F.)
  THE PASTORAL LETTER THERE REFERRED TO IS THIS ONE. It is the document the
  1874 House of Bishops treated as its own standing action on ritual. If any
  official body was going to characterize the ritual controversy in the register
  White & Dykman later used, this is where it would be.

  ⚠ CRITICAL QUALIFICATION ON THE 1874 MOTION: the Bishop of Maryland's
  substitute was NOT ADOPTED. The Journal records "Which was not adopted." Any
  citation of that "grave importance" language must cite it as a DEFEATED
  MOTION, not as the action or the mind of the House of Bishops. Recorded here
  because the phrasing is quotable and the error would be easy to make.

  ⚠ SECOND CORRECTION TO A COMMON ASSUMPTION: this Pastoral Letter is NOT
  printed in the 1871 Journal. It was published separately, which is why this
  capture is from a standalone pamphlet item rather than from the Journal. The
  only pastoral letter printed in the 1871 Journal is the one on the Chicago
  conflagration (printed pp. 63-65). Likewise the 1874 Pastoral Letter is not
  printed in the 1874 Journal, which records only a resolution "That the
  Secretary of this House take order for the publication of the Pastoral
  Letter."

§1. THE RITUAL SECTION — VERBATIM
    Printed pp. 6-8 (as reported; not independently verified).
================================================================================

"We counsel you to bear in mind, that, while, on the other hand, we must not
suffer ourselves to deny any real good, by reason of mere popular outcries
against ritual forms, so, on the other hand, we are never to allow professions
of self-denying labor and service, to blind us to the actual dangers of any
movement in the Church. What is known as *Ritualism* is mainly a question of
taste, temperament, and constitution, until it becomes the expression of
doctrine."

"The doctrine which chiefly attempts, as yet, to express itself by ritual, in
questionable and dangerous ways, is connected with the Holy Eucharist. That
doctrine is, emphatically, a novelty in theology..."

"...these are most certainly unauthorized by Holy Scripture... and most
dangerous in their tendencies... Wherefore, if a teacher suggests this error, by
act or posture, he places himself in antagonism to the doctrine of this Church,
and the teaching of GOD'S Word, and puts in peril the souls of men."

  [The letter's only use of "schism" concerns tract literature: publications
   "the whole aim of which is to undermine the legitimate authority of the chief
   pastors of the Church... and to lead to division and to schism."]

  [The letter's summary of the age's threats: "the peculiar dangers of our age
   are those of irreverence, lawlessness, and unbelief."]
