## What this document is, and what it is not

⛔⛔⛔ **THIS IS A TRANSLATION-MATCHING AID, NOT A NEW COMPILATION.** It is meant to
be read **alongside** printed pp. 44–48 of W. H. Frere's Appendix A ("Notes on the
Early History of the Use of Incense") in *The Case for Incense Submitted to His
Grace the Archbishop of Canterbury on behalf of the Rev. H. Westall* (1899) — leaves
56–60 of `src/SRC_PRIMARY_1899_Westall_Case_For_Incense-bwb_C0-AUU-939.pdf`, per the
`printed folio = leaf − 12` concordance established and verified in
`Ritualist_Case_For_Incense_and_the_1899_Opinion.md` §0.

⛔ **Every English translation quoted below was drawn from an existing, already-published
source — New Advent's reproduction of the Ante-Nicene Fathers (ANF) and the Nicene and
Post-Nicene Fathers (NPNF), 1885–88, revised/edited by Kevin Knight — and NONE was
produced, paraphrased, or touched by this pass.** ⛔⛔ **HARD RULE OBSERVED THROUGHOUT: no
Greek or Latin text was translated by this pass, not a full passage, not a phrase, not to
fill a gap.** Every quotation below is the translator's own published wording, copied
verbatim from its source page. Where no published translation could be located, the
passage is named but left untranslated in the UNMATCHED section, per brief.

⛔ Nothing here is a finding about Rev. James. He is not mentioned, quoted, or
characterised anywhere in this document, and nothing in it may be cited as evidence of
anything he holds, has said, or would say. It does not enter `SRC_Coverage_Register.md`
(§12 ruling — that register's subject is coverage of his own material). It does not touch
`Ritualist_Case_For_Incense_and_the_1899_Opinion.md` §2g, where Frere's own conclusions
are already evaluated — that is out of scope here.

⭐ **Overlap with prior passes.** Four of the nine passages Frere quotes had already been
verified against primary texts by `Patristic_Citations_Incense_Verification.md`
(`260835-47`) and `Tertullian_Incense_Passages.md` (`260835-48`) — the three Tertullian
passages (*Apology* 30, *Apology* 42, *De Corona* 10) and Athenagoras's *Plea* ch. 13.
Where a passage matched one of theirs (same work, same chapter/section), this pass reused
their same New Advent/CCEL source and translator rather than introducing a second
translation of the same passage from a different site. The remaining five passages
(Clement of Alexandria ×2, Origen ×2, Augustine ×1) were newly matched by this pass.

## The nine passages, in Frere's own order

### 1. Athenagoras, *Legatio* (*Plea for the Christians*) ch. 13 — printed p. 44

Frere's citation as printed: *"Legatio pro Christianis, § 13. Migne. P. G. vi. 916."*

> "And first, as to our not sacrificing: the Framer and Father of this universe does not
> need blood, nor the odour of burnt-offerings, nor the fragrance of flowers and incense,
> forasmuch as He is Himself perfect fragrance, needing nothing either within or without;
> but the noblest sacrifice to Him is for us to know who stretched out and vaulted the
> heavens... And what have I to do with holocausts, which God does not stand in need of?
> — though indeed it does behoove us to offer a bloodless sacrifice and 'the service of
> our reason.'"

**Source.** Translated by B. P. Pratten, *Ante-Nicene Fathers* vol. 2 (1885), ch. 13,
"Why the Christians Do Not Offer Sacrifices." New Advent:
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0205.htm>.

---

### 2. Tertullian, *Apologeticus* ch. 30 — printed p. 45

Frere's citation as printed: *"Apologeticus, § xxx. Migne. P. L. i. 442–444."*

> "Thither we lift our eyes, with hands outstretched, because free from sin; with head
> uncovered, for we have nothing whereof to be ashamed; finally, without a monitor,
> because it is from the heart we supplicate... offering to Him, at His own requirement,
> that costly and noble sacrifice of prayer dispatched from the chaste body, an unstained
> soul, a sanctified spirit, not the few grains of incense a farthing buys — tears of an
> Arabian tree — not a few drops of wine, — not the blood of some worthless ox..."

**Source.** Translated by S. Thelwall, *ANF* vol. 3 (1885), *Apology* ch. 30.
New Advent: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0301.htm>. CCEL (double-attested, per
`260835-47`/`48`): <https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/anf03.iv.iii.xxx.html>.

---

### 3. Tertullian, *Apologeticus* ch. 42 — printed p. 45

Frere's citation as printed: *"Apologeticus § xlii. P. L. i. 493."*

> "We certainly buy no frankincense. If the Arabias complain of this, let the Sabæans be
> well assured that their more precious and costly merchandise is expended as largely in
> the burying of Christians as in the fumigating of the gods."

**Source.** Translated by S. Thelwall, *ANF* vol. 3 (1885), *Apology* ch. 42.
New Advent: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0301.htm>. CCEL:
<https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/anf03.iv.iii.xlii.html>.

---

### 4. Tertullian, *De Corona* ch. 10 — printed p. 45

Frere's citation as printed: *"De Corona x. P. L. ii. 90."*

> "The substances are themselves as creatures of God without impurity, and in this their
> native state are free to the use of all; but the ministries to which in their use they
> are devoted, makes all the difference; for I, too, kill a cock for myself, just as
> Socrates did for Æsculapius; and if the smell of some place or other offends me, I burn
> the Arabian product myself, but not with the same ceremony, nor in the same dress, nor
> with the same pomp, with which it is done to idols."

**Source.** Translated by S. Thelwall, *ANF* vol. 3 (1885), *De Corona* ch. 10.
New Advent: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0304.htm>. CCEL:
<https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/anf03.iv.vi.x.html>.

---

### 5. Clement of Alexandria, *Stromata* vii. 6 [§ 32] — printed p. 46

Frere's citation as printed: *"Strom. vii. 6, [§ 32]. P. G. ix. 445."*

> "Now the very ancient altar in Delos they celebrated as holy; which alone, being
> undefiled by slaughter and death, they say Pythagoras approached. And will they not
> believe us when we say that the righteous soul is the truly sacred altar, and that
> incense arising from it is holy prayer?"

**Source.** Translated by William Wilson, *ANF* vol. 2 (1885), *The Stromata*, Book VII,
ch. 6, "Prayers and Praise from a Pure Mind, Ceaselessly Offered, Far Better Than
Sacrifices." New Advent: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02107.htm>.

---

### 6. Clement of Alexandria, *Paedagogus* ii. 8 [§ 67] — printed p. 46

Frere's citation as printed: *"Paedagogus ii. 8, [§ 67]. P. G. viii. 473."*

> "If, then, we say that the Lord the great High Priest offers to God the incense of
> sweet fragrance, let us not imagine that this is a sacrifice and sweet fragrance of
> incense; but let us understand it to mean, that the Lord lays the acceptable offering
> of love, the spiritual fragrance, on the altar."

**Source.** Translated by William Wilson, *ANF* vol. 2 (1885), *The Instructor*
(*Paedagogus*), Book II, ch. 8, "On the Use of Ointments and Crowns." New Advent:
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02092.htm>.

---

### 7. Origen, *Contra Celsum* viii. 17 — printed p. 46

Frere's citation as printed: *"Contra Celsum viii. 17. P. G. xi. 1541."* (quoting Apoc.
[Rev.] viii. and Ps. cxli., as Frere notes.)

> "He does not perceive that we regard the spirit of every good man as an altar from
> which arises an incense which is truly and spiritually sweet-smelling, namely, the
> prayers ascending from a pure conscience. Therefore it is said by John in the
> Revelation, 'The odours are the prayers of saints;' and by the Psalmist, 'Let my prayer
> come up before You as incense.'"

**Source.** Translated by Frederick Crombie, *ANF* vol. 4 (1885), *Contra Celsum*, Book
VIII, ch. 17. New Advent: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/04168.htm>.

---

### 8. Origen, *Contra Celsum* viii. 20 — printed p. 46

Frere's citation as printed: *"Ib. 20. P. G. xi. 1548."*

> "...there is no comparison between our statues and the statues of the heathen, nor
> between our altars, with what we may call the incense ascending from them, and the
> heathen altars, with the fat and blood of the victims..."

**Source.** Translated by Frederick Crombie, *ANF* vol. 4 (1885), *Contra Celsum*, Book
VIII, ch. 20. New Advent: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/04168.htm>.

---

### 9. Augustine, *Enarrationes in Psalmos*, on Ps. xlix.(l.) 14, § 21 — printed p. 47

Frere's citation as printed: *"Enarr. in ps. xlix. 21. P. L. xxxvi. 578"* (heading the
passage with the Vulgate text, *"Immola deo sacrificium laudis"* — Ps. 49[50]:14).

> "'Immolate to God the sacrifice of praise' [Psalm 49:14]... We are without anxiety, we
> go not into Arabia in quest of frankincense: not any bags of covetous dealer do we
> sift: God requires of us the sacrifice of praise."

**Source.** Translated by J. E. Tweed, *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, First Series,
vol. 8 (1888), *Expositions on the Psalms*, on Psalm 49 [Vulgate numbering; New Advent's
own page title follows the Hebrew/AV number, "Psalm 50"], § 21. New Advent:
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801050.htm>. ⚠️ Note for anyone re-locating this:
New Advent titles this page "Exposition on Psalm 50" while its internal Bible
cross-reference links read "Psalm 49" — both point at the same Vulgate Ps. 49(50), the
psalm Frere cites; verified directly against the text at § 21, which reproduces Frere's
Latin ("Securi sumus... non imus in Arabiam thus quaerere... sacrificium laudis quaerit a
nobis Deus") word for word in translation.

## Not in scope — bare citations and paraphrase, no original-language text to match

Two further items appear on printed pp. 46–47 that are **not** Greek/Latin quotations and
so are not translation-matching candidates at all; they are recorded here so their
absence above is not mistaken for an oversight.

- **Printed p. 46 — Arnobius and Lactantius.** Frere writes, in his own English prose and
  without quoting either author's original language: *"Arnobius at a still later date
  discusses the point at length, and argues, in reply to the heathen objector, that the
  use of incense among the Romans was novel, and in itself senseless and inapplicable to
  Divine beings... Similar treatment is found in Lactantius."* His footnotes cite
  *Arnobius, Adv. Gentes* vii. 26–28 (P. L. v. 1253–1258) and *Lactantius, Div. Instit.*
  vi. 25 (P. L. vi. 730) as the sources for that summary, but quote neither. There is
  nothing here in Greek or Latin to match against a translation.
- **Printed p. 47 — the Warren footnote.** A footnote citing F. E. Warren, *Liturgy of
  the Ante-Nicene Church*, p. 130, itself referring to *Didache* xiv.; Justin Martyr,
  *Dialog. cum Tryphone* 28, 41, 116, 117; Irenaeus, *Haer.* iv. 17, 18; Tertullian, *Adv.
  Judaeos* 5; *Adv. Marcionem* iii. 22. This is a bare bibliography of further citations
  with no quoted text attached — a pointer to Warren's own work, not a quotation Frere is
  presenting for the reader.
